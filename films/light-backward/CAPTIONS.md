[open] 10:02 PM tonight. Your back garden. / That's a second Sun. / And it will rise every evening for billions of years.

[title] What if light started moving backward?

[before] 9:59 PM. Three minutes earlier. / A clear night. The stars, a bright Moon, your cat.

[rule] At exactly 10:00, every ray of light in the universe turns around. / Just once. / After that, lamps, fires and the Sun shine as normal.

[vanish] 10:00:00. / Every star goes out. And the Moon.

[crescent] Then a thin sliver of Moon lights up. On the wrong side.

[old] But starlight is years old. / And all of it is now heading back to the stars.

[sirius] 10:00:20. The brightest star in the sky appears, in the west. / It's Sirius. It shouldn't rise for 2 more hours, in the east.

[mirror] Starlight that had already passed the Earth is coming back. / So every star appears on the opposite side of the sky. / Back to front.

[replay] And your lantern? Its light turned around too. / Slowed down a billion times, it pours back into the flame. / A few billionths of a second, and it's over.

[canopus] 10:00:57. Canopus, the second brightest star of all. / You can't see it from Britain. Ever. Until now.

[north] 10:01. The northern sky starts to glow. / Like dawn. But dawn is 9 hours away.

[switch] 10:01:20. / A second Sun switches on.

[day] Daylight. At 10 PM. / The robins start singing. / The streetlights switch off.

[why] All the sunlight that ever shot past the Earth turned around. / It's flooding back towards the Sun. / And the Earth is in the way.

[shadow] But right behind the Earth, there was a gap: its own shadow. / The gap came back too. / It took 80 seconds for the Earth to slide out of it.

[wave] From space, you'd see the light sweep across the night side. / 60 kilometres every second. / In 3 and a half minutes, the whole night side is lit.

[moonwhy] That's what lit the Moon's dark side, too.

[project] Your telescope throws its image onto a card. / Its sunspots are back to front. / It's the Sun in a mirror.

[past] This Sun is made of old light. / Right now, it shows the Sun as it was at 9:49 PM. / And every second, it shows a second further back.

[hawaii] And on the day side? At 10:00, it was noon in Hawaii. / Their Sun went out.

[black] The sky turned black. / Stars at noon. / The beach went dark.

[half] Not just in Hawaii. / On half the planet, the Sun went out at the same moment.

[wait] Sunlight takes 8 minutes 16 seconds to get here. / So nothing could arrive until the Sun sent more.

[voyager] 25 billion kilometres away, Voyager 1 was talking to Earth. / Radio is light too. Its signal, almost a day old, turned around. / NASA won't hear from it until tomorrow night.

[return] 10:08:16. / The Sun comes back. A dot first, then a disc.

[double] Then it gets brighter. And brighter. / The Sun is being hit by all its own old light, coming back. / Its surface heats up from 5,500 to 6,600 °C. / It shines twice as bright.

[full] 10:08:18. Back home, the Moon lights up again. / One side is twice as bright as the other.

[three] Earth now gets 3 times as much sunlight. / More than Venus.

[midnight] Midnight. The second Sun stands high in the south. / Higher than the real Sun ever gets in October. / Your neighbour's solar panels are working.

[sunrise] 6:56 AM. The real Sun rises in the east, / at the same moment the second Sun sets in the west. / For a few minutes, you have two shadows.

[noon] Noon. The Sun is twice as bright, and whiter. / You'd get sunburnt 3 times faster.

[never] And it never gets dark again. / Not here. Not anywhere on Earth. / Owls, bats and moths lose the dark for good.

[space] From space, the Earth has no night side. / Just a thin dim ring, where both suns are setting.

[summer] Every day, Britain now gets more sunlight / than it used to get on midsummer's day. / Winter never comes.

[heat] Earth is heating up hundreds of times faster / than it is with climate change today. / The Arctic sea ice is gone within months.

[boil] Scientists think 10% more sunlight could one day boil the oceans away. / This is 200% more. / Within about a thousand years, the oceans are gone.

[birth] And the second Sun keeps running backwards through time. / Billions of years from now, it will show the Sun being born. / Only then will the night come back.

[real] Is any of this real?

[real2] Light really does take time to reach you. / Moonlight is 1.3 seconds old. Sunlight, about 8 minutes. / Most starlight you see tonight left before you were born.

[real3] And the sunlight that missed the Earth is still out there. / Billions of years of it, flying away.

[real4] Scientists really can turn light around. / A phase-conjugate mirror sends a beam back exactly the way it came. / In 2023, a lab even built a "time mirror" for microwaves.

[real5] But nothing could turn all the light in the universe around. / You'd have to flip the magnetic field of every light wave at once.

[question] If it happened tonight at 10, what would you look at at 9:59?
