# Episode report — *What if light started moving backward?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 7:49 (468.9 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Slower sound
formula (the brief: that formula, grounded and escalating, about 8 minutes with no filler, never implying more than
the physics). The film is told by 106 short second-person captions, a corner readout (SINCE LIGHT TURNED AROUND · the
time · what is back · NEXT landmark and its time), a clock (TONIGHT / TOMORROW / IN HAWAII), a context line, labels,
honest tags, a few illustrations and the sound. Page `light-backward.html`, code `films/light-backward/`, every number
in `PLAN.md` (computed in `physics.js`), captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The idea.** At exactly 10:00 PM tonight every ray of light in the universe turns around, once; after that lamps,
fires and the Sun shine as normal. Light in flight retraces its path, so you start receiving the light that had
already passed you, sent back: first nothing (every star and the Moon go out), then the light the Earth itself had
sent up into space (town lights in the sky, upside down; in Hawaii at noon, the sea and the clouds), then mirror-image
stars, and at 10:01:21 a second Sun: all the sunlight that ever shot past the Earth, coming back. It never sets for
good. The real Sun goes out on the day side for 8 minutes and comes back twice as bright. Then: three times the
sunlight, no more night, the oceans boil within a few thousand years, and the second Sun keeps running back through
time. Is any of it real, and a question for the comments.

## Reviews

**Round 1** (first preview, 7:37; three independent reviewers, scores as given):
- Viewer: retention 6/10, clarity 6/10. The second Sun, Hawaii going dark and the doubled Sun land; the mirror stars,
  "the gap came back", the phase-conjugate mirror and the magnetic-field line got lost; 0:38–1:42 was a minute of
  near-black sky; the trigger moment was invisible.
- Visuals: 6/10. The mirror sky never visibly appeared; the Moon was never seen going out; the Sun's return was rushed
  and washed out; the space stream read as TV static; the boil read as a snowball; floating slivers in the night garden.
- Physics: 7/10. The big miss: the day side's own reflected sunlight was also in flight, so Hawaii's sky could not snap
  to black, and the night sky gets the town lights back. Also: the rule needed a frame, the boil time was too short.
- Fixed: the mirror Earth everywhere (town lights at 10:00:08, Moscow from above, Hawaii's sky full of upside-down sea,
  the little Earth at dawn); the stars and the Moon going out as one beat; the stream, the boil and the return redrawn.

**Round 2** (second preview, 7:55; a physics reviewer, and one reviewer for retention, clarity and visuals):
- Physics: **8.5/10**. Re-derived the core model and confirmed nearly every number (10:00:08, 81.03 s, Sirius at
  20.5 s, 495.85 s, sunrise 6:44:38, the little Earth, 476 W/m², 47 days of ice, the second Sun lasting). Asked for:
  the Sun's ×2 as an upper limit (a convective surface may settle at ×1.5–1.9), "sunrise is almost 9 hours away", Sirius
  "nearly 2 more hours, in the south-east", the 30 vs 60 km/s gap explained, the star count limited to mag 5, the long
  term ×3 (not 2.3), the mirror Earth 2–2.5× brighter. All done.
- Viewer: retention **6/10**, clarity **6/10**, visuals **6/10**. No "before" for 10:00 (the "bright Moon" was a
  streetlamp); 68 s of murky grey sky; an unlabelled giant arc; washed-out day shots and the Sun's return in the wrong
  shot; a third rewind in Hawaii and a garden-time NEXT line there; captions claiming what the picture didn't show;
  labels on top of their subjects; too much HUD.
- Fixed: a wide starry sky with the Moon and the cat before 10:00, then a hard cut to black as the sound drops away; brighter,
  sparkling town lights and Moscow named in the caption; the arc kept out of the cold open and labelled once; the Sun's
  return held in one telephoto shot through a solar filter with a brightness readout climbing to ×1.99; the beach and
  the day shots re-graded; no rewind for the half planet; a plate behind the clock; a soft caption band; the Moon's
  sides labelled ×2/×1; the little Earth brighter; labels moved off the suns and the shadows; a summer day-lapse; the
  real section rebuilt (one illustration, one lab inset); "up to" on every ×2/×3 claim; three captions cut. 7:55 → 7:49.
- Not re-reviewed after these fixes (no third round), so the 6s above are for the previous cut.

## What is still weak

- **Europe is not recognisable.** The upside-down town lights read as lights, not as a map; the caption and label name
  Moscow instead.
- **The Moon's two sides (×2 and ×1) don't visibly differ.** The thin night-side sliver near the limb is as bright per
  pixel as the day side (a dusty surface looks brightest at grazing angles); the labels carry the point.
- **The second Sun's shadow at sunrise is faint** (it is the weaker sun, low in the west, so this is correct, but the
  "two shadows" moment is subtle).
- **The beach in the doubled sunlight** is better graded but still a plain shot.
- **The real-sky date.** The night is a generic late October. On the real 26 Oct 2026 the Moon is full and Saturn and
  Jupiter are up; the film's Moon is a waxing gibbous and the planets are left out.
