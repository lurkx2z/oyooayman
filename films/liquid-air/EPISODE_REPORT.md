# Episode report — *What if the air turned into a breathable liquid for one hour?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 5:52 (352.5 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Friction 10s
formula, built to the owner's YouTube lessons and retention playbook of 28 October 2026 and the rule of 29 October
2026: **the story clock only runs forward**. It runs at real speed through the first minute, the dive under, the
crash and the sun, the bell and the last ten seconds, faster in between, and it only jumps forward at a cut (a
"+N MIN" flag on the HUD clock). The film is told by 67 short second-person captions, a corner readout (THE AIR,
TURNED TO LIQUID %, the level in your street, and from 3:15 the countdown IT TURNS BACK INTO AIR IN 45 MIN), one
context line per scene, a clock (SATURDAY 3:07:58 PM), pinned labels, one diagram, an honest tag and the sound.
Page `liquid-air.html`, code `films/liquid-air/`, every number in `PLAN.md` § 2 (computed in `physics.js` and a 1-D
column model of the falling sky run once offline, `build-column.cjs` → `column.js`), captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The physical reading.** At 3:00:00 on a Saturday afternoon all the air on Earth starts turning into a clear liquid,
the same amount every second, all of it by 3:10:00; at 4:00 it turns back the same way. The liquid stays at 15 °C and
is as dense as liquid air (875 kg/m³), and it carries oxygen you can breathe. **That is the one impossible thing**
(real air is liquid only at −194 °C; the film says so). Breathing an oxygen-rich liquid is real (mice, 1966). Then:

- the sky weighs 10.3 t on every m², so as this liquid it is **11.8 m** deep; a litre of it is 714 litres of air;
- it forms everywhere at once, so it rains in your room, and kilometres of rain stand between you and the sun:
  **the sun is gone within a second** (the street lights switch on at 3:03); outside it rains 4× the world record;
- the rain holds the sky up: the pressure at the street stays near 1 atm until 3:09 (0.70 atm), then the last of the
  gas goes and **61 % of the sky is still falling: it lands in ten seconds** (3:10:00–3:10:10, ~130 m/s);
- your ears hurt (the air behind your eardrums turns too); sealed things shrink; footballs and tyres go soft;
- parked cars float at 70 cm, drift, fill and sink; in it you weigh 17.5 % (12 kg);
- from under the surface the whole sky is squeezed into a circle 56° from straight up (**Snell's window**; outside it
  the surface is a mirror); after 3:10:12 nothing is left up there: **the sun in a black sky, with stars**;
- your flat (12.0 m up) is just above the surface: **in a vacuum**: your glass of water boils at 15 °C, then freezes;
- wood floats up (for now), bins don't, water is heavier (the pond stays a pond), pigeons fly in it, the church bell
  strikes in a vacuum (heard only through the stone), the top 70 cm is past the Armstrong limit (blood would boil);
- every breath of a 15 °C liquid pulls heat out of your chest; breathing it is slow, hard work;
- **4:00**: it turns back, every litre giving 1.2 litres of gas a second: after one second there's more gas than
  liquid, the street turns to a white froth, and the 3 litres in your lungs make a full breath of air every second.
  Breathe out. We end five seconds in: *Would you have breathed it?*

## How it follows the owner's lessons and playbook

- Everyday place, human scale: your living room on the fifth floor and the street below; your neighbours.
- Visual action in the first seconds: drops form out of nothing under the title.
- Real nuance in the first minute: real liquid air is −194 °C; liquid breathing was done in 1966.
- No "X kept getting slower" derivative; no slow science recap at the end; the film ends 5 s into 4:00.
- No clock resets: one forward clock, jumps only at cuts, "+N MIN" flags; the count to 4:00 runs at real speed.
- About six minutes (5:52), no filler; three signature moments (rain in your room and the sun going; the crash from
  under the surface into a black sky; Snell's window and the 4:00 froth).
- Tension to the last frame: from 3:21 the HUD counts down to 4:00 and the caption plants what it will do to you.
- Teaser Shorts: cut from 2:59–3:18 (the crash and the sun) and 5:36–5:52 (the count and the froth).

## Reviews

Round 1, three independent reviewers on 1 fps stills of the whole film, before the fixes. Scores as given:

- **Retention** 5.5/10 overall (hook 5, first 30 s 5, middle 7, payoff 5, clarity 6). Swipe points: 0:04–0:10 (rain
  reads as ordinary rain), 0:20–0:39 (static; the sun still visible under "the sun's gone"), 3:20–3:31 (a calm
  explainer right after the climax), 3:48–4:00 (Snell's window looked like a repeat), 4:20–4:32 (the pond).
  Top problem: no stakes for two minutes after the crash.
- **Visuals**: image 6, composition 5, phone readability 5, underwater 6.5, hero moments 5 (the sun in a black sky
  lands, the crash half-lands, the room going dark didn't). Cheapest: blocky foreground hands, the packet, the tower,
  the pond, tiny HUD boxes.
- **Physics**: accuracy 7, honesty 6, clarity 8, learning 8, believability 6. Ten errors and overstatements (all in
  PLAN.md § 7), the biggest: the 4:00 fizz far too gentle, sealed cars can't float, "5 times" (4×), 2,000 vs 2,140.

Fixed after round 1 (all reviewers' top three, plus most of the rest; details in PLAN.md § 7): the sun disc (it was a
lamp's highlight on the window glass) and the darkening; the 4:00 threat planted at 3:21 with a HUD countdown;
drops visibly forming as beads; a new Snell's window shot straight up; the 4:00 froth and whiteout with its roar; no
sealed floating cars; eleven caption corrections; the girl's face; the float, pond and two street shots reframed;
ice in the glass; a jolt at the slam; bigger HUD boxes. No second scoring round was run.

## What is still weak

- The crash from below shows spray blotches, not columns of mass arriving or the surface being driven down.
- The crisp packet still reads as a red blob; the extras' poses are stiff (arms out); the bell tower is a box.
- The physics reviewer's bigger points are lines, not consequences: the cold (breathing it would chill you by
  ~0.3–0.5 °C a minute: severe by 3:40), CO₂, and the crash wrecking roofs, trees and the tower top above 4.6 m
  (not shown); the left-out heat of the fall (~40 °C) is a HUD tag.
- The pond shot is still murky; the second half is still a run of facts between the countdown lines.
