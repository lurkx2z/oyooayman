[title] What if all men suddenly disappeared?

[start] 7:42 AM. Every man and boy on Earth just vanished. / 4.1 billion people. Half of everyone alive.

[wheel] Your dad was driving. The car is still doing 100 km/h. / The cruise control doesn't know he's gone.

[brake] Grab the wheel. Hold the parking-brake switch. / In most modern cars, that's an emergency stop.

[lorry] The lorry ahead had a man at the wheel too. / It drifts across your lane, into the barrier.

[cab] Its cab is empty, and the wheel turns by itself. / Almost every lorry driver in Europe is a man.

[stop] You stop 3 m from it.

[cars] All along the viaduct, cars with no one at the wheel. / Some newer ones notice nobody is steering, and stop themselves. / The older ones drift until something stops them.

[radio] The radio is still playing. A computer picks the songs. / The machines haven't noticed yet.

[phone] His phone, on the floor. MUM. / Everyone is calling someone who's gone. The network jams.

[train] Down by the river, a train is slowing down on its own. / Its driver had to keep a pedal pressed. When he let go, the brakes came on.

[barge] A barge on autopilot holds its course. / The river bends. The barge doesn't.

[plane] High above you, a plane at 11,000 m. / Flight 2207. Both its pilots were men. / The autopilot is still flying it.

[world] About 9,000 airliners are in the air right now. / 19 in 20 airline pilots are men. In 9 of every 10 cockpits, no one is left.

[door] In the cabin, a flight attendant at the cockpit door. / It's locked from inside. Her emergency code opens it, but only after 30 seconds. / No one inside can answer. / She has never flown a plane.

[alone] She's alone up front, with 3 hours 20 minutes of fuel. / 64 people behind her. Half the seats just emptied.

[tower] The airport by the motorway. Its control tower had 9 controllers this morning. 2 are left. / Every plane in range is calling them.

[smoke] 7:51 AM. Smoke over the town. / Breakfast was on the stove in thousands of kitchens. / 9 in 10 firefighters were men.

[ward] 8:04 AM. The town's hospital, the maternity ward. / Almost every midwife on Earth is a woman. They're all still here. / A baby is born. A boy. / He's still here. It only took the ones already born. / About 2 boys are born every second. 50 million more are on their way.

[walk] 9:10 AM. Nothing on the motorway has moved. / No police, no tow trucks. Most of them were men too. / People start walking home.

[alert] 9:31 AM. An emergency alert finally gets through. / It's from a deputy minister. Her boss, and his boss, are gone. / In 166 of 193 countries, the top leaders were all men.

[hold] 9:47 AM. Flight 2207 is circling the airport, with 1 hour 17 minutes of fuel left.

[many] 24 planes are circling now. / Talking one pilot-less plane down takes about 15 minutes. / The last would land in 6 hours. Most have 2.

[autoland] So they try something else. Most airliners can land themselves. / Someone just has to set it up: the runway's radio beam, the flaps, the wheels. / The tower reads it out, one switch at a time. / Three autopilots lock on to the beam.

[final] 10:21 AM. Flight 2207 comes in over the motorway. / Its wheels are down. Nobody is touching the controls. / 1 km to go. 260 km/h. / In the tower, they can only watch. / It lands itself.

[question] 23 planes are still up there, flying themselves. / Who will talk them all down?
