[title] What if the speed of light increased and couldn't stop?

[start] 9:00 PM. Light just started speeding up. / 1% faster every second. And it never stops.

[gps] Your phone just lost its GPS. / GPS works by timing light from space, and its maths still uses the old speed.

[real] Officially, this is impossible. / Since 1983 the speed of light has been a fixed number, 299,792,458 m/s. The metre is defined by it.

[magnet] But light is electricity and magnetism, rippling. / Speed light up, and every magnet on Earth gets weaker. Twice as fast, four times weaker.

[flicker] ×1.08. The grid's transformers start to choke. Every light on the coast flickers.

[mri] Across the bay, the hospital's MRI scanner is a magnet made of superconducting wire. / To hold its field as magnetism weakens, the current in the wire climbs on its own. / ×1.1. The wire can't carry it. / Its liquid helium boils off in a white plume.

[blackout] ×1.15. The grid gives up. / The stadium goes dark with 40,000 people inside. / Then 40,000 phones light up.

[generator] In the hospital, the backup generators start. They're magnets too. / Only the battery lights stay on.

[brake] Your train runs on the grid. The driver brakes. / From 120 km/h, in the dark.

[road] ×1.3. Cars stall. Their spark coils and fuel injectors are magnets.

[mercury] On the mountain, the observatory's old barometer. / Mercury is liquid at room temperature only because of relativity. / Faster light undoes that. ×1.32, at 6 °C: it starts to freeze.

[ring] Gold is yellow only because of relativity too. / ×1.5. Your ring is almost silver.

[beach] The train stops by a beach. / There's a fire down there, and a party. Their music keeps getting quieter.

[speaker] A speaker is a magnet pushing a cone. / ×2. It pushes a quarter as hard.

[board] In the hospital, the patients' names are sliding off the board.

[fire] Look at the fire. / Hot things glow by shaking light loose. Faster light is harder to shake loose. / ×2.7. The fire is seven times dimmer, and no cooler.

[waves] The waves were glowing with plankton. / That light is fading, like every phone screen.

[phones] ×3. In the stadium, 40,000 phones are going dark.

[colour] Then the colours start to go. / Colour is molecules catching light. Faster light is harder to catch. / ×5. Your eyes are molecules too.

[day] It's daytime on the other side of the Earth. / The sunlight already on its way is arriving faster. Six times the heat, barely brighter. / 9:03 PM. That old sunlight has all arrived. What the Sun sends now is far weaker.

[heat] ×10. Earth sheds its heat into space as light. Only 1% gets out now. / From tonight, the Earth can't cool down.

[stars] Only one light isn't fading. / The stars. Their light left them years ago, and it's arriving faster and faster. / So they still look as bright as ever.

[runout] But that light is running out. / It was already on its way. When the last of it arrives, a star goes dark.

[saturn] 9:07 PM. Saturn goes out. / Its light was sunlight that left before 9:00. It has all arrived.

[stretch] 9:10 PM. Light's waves are stretching too. They're 400 times longer. / Your eyes can't focus them any more. Every star is a blur.

[pupil] 9:16 PM. The waves are now as wide as your pupils. / Soon almost none of it will fit through.

[question] What will be the last light you ever see?
