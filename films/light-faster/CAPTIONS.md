[title] What if the speed of light increased and couldn't stop?

[start] 9:00 PM. Light just started speeding up. / 1% faster every second. And it never stops.

[gps] Your phone thinks you're out at sea. / GPS works by timing light from space, and its maths still uses the old speed.

[real] Officially, this is impossible. / Since 1983 the speed of light is a fixed number, 299,792,458 m/s. The metre is defined by it. / Every electron, every atom and gravity stay exactly as they are. Only light speeds up.

[magnet] But light and magnetism are one force. / Speed light up, and every magnet on Earth gets weaker. Twice as fast, four times weaker.

[flicker] ×1.08. The iron in the grid's transformers can't hold the magnetism they need.

[mri] Across the bay, the hospital's MRI scanner is a magnet made of superconducting wire. / To hold its field as magnetism weakens, the current in the wire climbs on its own. / ×1.1. The wire can't carry it. / Its liquid helium boils off in seconds.

[blackout] ×1.15. The grid gives up. / The stadium goes dark with 40,000 people inside. / Then 40,000 phones light up.

[generator] In the hospital, the backup generators start. They're magnets too. / Only the battery lights stay on.

[brake] Your train runs on the grid. The driver brakes. / The emergency lights run on batteries.

[road] ×1.3. Cars stall: the coil that makes their spark is a magnet. / Their batteries are dying anyway. 10 of their 12 volts come from relativity.

[mercury] On the mountain, the observatory's old barometer. / Mercury is a liquid only because of relativity: its inner electrons move at more than half the speed of light. / Faster light makes that matter less. ×1.32, at 6 °C: it freezes.

[ring] Gold is yellow for the same reason. / ×1.5. Your ring has turned silver.

[beach] The train stops by a beach. / There's a fire down there, and a party. Their music keeps getting quieter.

[speaker] A speaker is a magnet pushing a cone. / ×2. It pushes a quarter as hard.

[board] In the hospital, every magnet holds a quarter as hard. / The patients' names slide off the board.

[fire] Look at the fire. / Hot things glow by shaking light loose. Faster light is harder to shake loose. / ×2.7. The fire is seven times dimmer, and just as hot.

[waves] The waves were glowing with plankton. / They make light the way your phone screen does. Both are fading.

[phones] ×3. In the stadium, 40,000 phones are going dark.

[colour] Then the colours start to go. / Colour is molecules catching light. Faster light is harder to catch. / ×5. Your eyes are molecules too.

[day] It's daytime on the other side of the Earth. / The sunlight already on its way is arriving faster. By 9:03, six times the heat. / It barely looks brighter. It burns six times hotter.

[heat] ×10. Earth sheds its heat into space as light. It can't any more. / Tonight is the first night in history that won't get colder.

[stars] Only one light isn't fading. / The stars. Their light left them years ago, and it's arriving faster and faster. / Just fast enough to make up for your eyes.

[runout] But that light is running out. / Every second, faster light swallows a longer stretch of its own past.

[saturn] 9:07 PM. Saturn goes out. / Its light was sunlight that left before 9:00. It has all arrived.

[nearest] 9:23 PM. Light is 1.4 million times faster. / The nearest star just went out. / The rest will follow, nearest first.

[question] What will be the last light you ever see?
