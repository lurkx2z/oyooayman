[title] What if the ocean kept rising and couldn't stop?

[start] 6:42 PM. High tide. It should turn now. It doesn't. / The sea is rising 1 metre every minute. And it isn't going to stop. / You're the keeper of a lighthouse on a rock, 22 km off the coast of Maine.

[boat] Your boat is tied to the landing. Its rope is too short for this. / The rope won't let it rise, so the sea pours in. / The knot is already under water. No way off the rock now.

[phone] Your daughter Isla is in town with her mum, on the coast, 22 km away. / You tell her to go up the hill. Now.

[rule] To rise 1 metre, the oceans need 361,000 cubic kilometres of new water. / All the rivers on Earth would take 10 years to bring that much. / It's coming every minute. From nowhere.

[height] The lamp at the top of your tower is 47 metres above high tide. / At a metre a minute, you have 45 minutes.

[lichen] Orange lichen only grows above the highest tides. The sea is over it now.

[radio] On the coastguard radio, every harbour on the coast is calling for help. / You call for help too. Nobody answers. They're all calling.

[bell] The bell buoy out there is chained to the sea floor. Its chain just ran out.

[world] One person in ten on Earth lives less than 10 metres above the sea. / The sea just passed 10 metres.

[fog] The fog signal has warned ships off this rock for 120 years. / You sound it one last time. The sea drowns it.

[cottage] Your house. The sea comes in under the door. / In 3 minutes, it fills the ground floor.

[island] There's no island any more. Just the tower, and the top of your house. / The swell breaks where the island was.

[burst] It gives.

[dark] The power room floods. Every light in the tower goes out. / All but the lamp. It runs on its own sealed battery, at the top.

[stairs] The sea is coming up the stairs behind you. A step every 11 seconds. / It doesn't need to be fast. It never stops. It has 105 steps to go.

[town] Isla: the roads are jammed, so they're walking up the hill. / Then every light in her town goes out.

[lights] Up and down the coast, the other lighthouses are going dark. / Yours is the last lighthouse still lit.

[ship] Out at sea, a ship just floats up with it. Tonight, the safest place on Earth is a boat. / Yours is 31 metres down, still tied to the rock. / You call the ship. It turns for you. It needs 50 minutes.

[hill] Isla made it to the top of the hill. / From up there, 24 km away, she can see your light.

[lie] You tell her you're fine.

[watch] The watch room floods. The radio dies. / You climb up into the lamp.

[gallery] No rock. No island. Just the light, on the open sea.
