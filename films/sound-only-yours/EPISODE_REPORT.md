# Episode report — *What if every sound vanished except yours?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 5:09 (309.3 s). No narrator: the only voice is the main character's
own (ten short lines made with ElevenLabs, voice "Liam", `VO-you.md`), because his is the only voice left in the
world. Built for the mood the owner asked for on 2 November 2026 (the Slower light comments: "scary, like a horror
game"), to the owner's YouTube lessons of 28 October 2026 (an everyday place, action in the first seconds, a
real-physics nod in the first minute, five to six minutes, tension to the last frame) and the rule of 29 October 2026:
**the story clock only runs forward**. One clock runs from Thursday 9:41 PM to 10:10 PM, at real speed, in slow
motion once (the glass, ×0.35), and it only jumps forward at a cut ("+N s"). The film is told by 68 short
second-person captions, the clock, a counter (COULD HAVE HEARD YOU), a sound meter (your sound, its level, how far it
carries), one context line per beat, labels pinned in the scene, honest tags, your voice and the score. Page
`sound-only-yours.html`, code `films/sound-only-yours/`, every number in `PLAN.md` § 2 (computed in `physics.js`),
captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The physical reading.** From 9:41:00 PM every sound wave you did not start dies the instant it starts: voices, rain,
engines, speakers, animals, alarms, thunder. Yours stay: your voice, breath, heartbeat, steps, claps, clicks, and every
echo of them. **That is the one impossible part** (the HUD says so). Everything else follows:

- sound is air being pushed: your pushes still travel at 337 m/s and fade as always (spreading, plus 0.002 dB/m of air
  absorption), and with nothing to mask them they carry far: your voice about 500 m, a shout about 5 km, if nothing is
  in the way (behind shut windows a shout carries about 750 m; the captions say "up to" and "could have");
- with nothing else to hear, your body gets loud (the quietest room on Earth, −20 dB); a ringing creeps in;
- a phone call connects and carries nothing (a speaker's sound isn't yours);
- every warning made of sound fails: a silent car passes 1.2 m in front of you (why new EVs in Europe must hum below
  20 km/h: tagged), an ambulance's siren that nobody hears;
- lightning 2 km away, counted to six, and the thunder never comes;
- echoes are yours too: the tower block 170 m away answers your shout in 1.01 s; in the dark you can see with your own
  clicks (stylised, and slowed about 67×: tagged), and so can everyone who hears them, 1.6 km away.

## How it follows the owner's lessons and playbook

- Everyday place, human scale: your 5th-floor kitchen on a rainy Thursday night, your stairwell, your street, the park
  across the road.
- Action on frame 1: the glass goes over the edge under the title, and the world goes silent at 0.7 s; a hook at 0:16
  (somebody heard you).
- Real nuance in the first minute: "Sound is just air being pushed. Only your pushes still count." (0:30), the
  quietest room on Earth (0:40).
- Not an "X kept getting slower" derivative; no clock resets (one forward clock, every cut moves the night on).
- Five minutes (5:09) without a recap; escalation from strange (the glass, the clap) to lonely (Mum) to unnerving (the
  windows, the man on the stairs) to dangerous (the silent car, the ambulance) to frightening (the shout, the crowd
  walking towards it) to horror (the dark, the clicks, the one behind you).
- Tension to the last frame: the figure behind you, cut on the question.
- The horror feel the owner asked for: an original eerie score that leads, hard silences (the world's, then the
  score's own: four seconds of nothing after "It never comes"), the tinnitus, your own breath and heartbeat.
- Teaser Shorts: the windows lighting up (1:20–1:30), the stairwell turn (1:49–2:06), the last click (4:58–5:09).

## Sound

All synthesised and seeded except your voice (`sound-*.js`, `mix.js`), baked into `soundtrack.js` with the shared
fingerprint check (so the ZIP plays its sound outside Chrome too). Three stems:

- **music**: an original eerie score that leads the whole way (a music-box lullaby in D minor, glassy clusters, a sub,
  backwards notes where a sound should be, risers that stop dead, hits on the scares); it goes silent with the world
  where silence is the point (before your clap, after "It never comes"). Nothing in it imitates a sound the world has
  lost (no siren wail, no thunder roll, no footstep march).
- **amb**: the world for its last 0.7 s (rain on the window, the news on the TV, the fridge, the cat purring, a car on
  the wet street), cut dead mid-word; then only what is inside you: your blood's rush (loud once "your body gets loud")
  and a tinnitus thread that comes back in every long silence.
- **sfx**: your voice (the ten lines) in the acoustics of where you are, your claps, steps, breath and heartbeat (its
  rate follows the story), and every echo the physics allows (the flats across the park at 0.62 s; the shout's five
  buildings and their second bounces; the count off the flats), your clicks and what they find (a tree, a bench,
  something soft, the crowd, the one behind you).

**Mix.** −15.8 LUFS integrated, −1.9 dBTP, loudness range 6.9 LU (the baked WAV); the mastered `final-soundtrack.m4a`
(AAC 256k, a limiter at −3 dB) measures −16.0 LUFS and −2.0 dBTP. **The score leads** (the owner's rule after Days
shorter): measured solo, music −17.7 LUFS, beds −33.3 LUFS; stem by stem in 10-second windows across the whole film the
music sits 10.6 to 35 dB over the beds (median 17.9 dB, unweighted RMS), the closest moments being "your body gets loud"
(0:40) and the dark (4:30), where your own blood is meant to be heard. Stem trims music +6, beds −8, effects +4 dB
(`mix.js`).
