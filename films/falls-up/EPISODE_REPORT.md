# Episode report — *What if everything you dropped fell up for 10 minutes?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 4:58 (298.0 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Friction 10s / Days
shorter formula, built to the owner's YouTube lessons of 28 October 2026 (an everyday place, action in the first
seconds, a real-physics nod in the first minute, about five to six minutes, tension to the last frame) and the rule of
29 October 2026: **the story clock only runs forward**. One clock runs from Saturday 2:59:59 PM to 3:10:25 PM, at
real speed, in slow motion for six moments ("SLOW MOTION ×0.25" on the HUD), and it only jumps forward at a cut
("+N s"). The film is told by 55 short second-person captions, a corner readout (DROPPED THINGS FALL UP FOR 9:52; from
3:01:30 your apple's height), the clock, one context line per beat, labels pinned in the scene, two insets (walking
against running, the height ladder), honest tags and the sound. Page `falls-up.html`, code `films/falls-up/`, every
number in `PLAN.md` § 2 (computed in `physics.js`), captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The physical reading.** From 3:00:00 to 3:10:00 PM, anything that loses its support falls **up** at 9.81 m/s²
(weaker with height) until something stops it, and whatever it lands on holds it there. The air keeps its weight (or
the sky would leave), and anything still held or standing is untouched. **That, and gravity choosing what to push, is
the impossible part**: between masses gravity only ever pulls (the film says so at 0:25). Everything else follows:

- Galileo's rule still holds: without air in the way, everything falls at the same rate (a spoon and a grape hit your
  ceiling together, 0.57 s after you let go); the glass hits it 0.56 s after it slips;
- the tap drips up into the cupboard over it; the tea pours up and spreads into a puddle on the ceiling;
- the cat jumps down from the counter and falls up: falling up feels exactly like falling down, so it has to see the
  ceiling coming to know which way to turn; it twists and lands on its feet, and every jump "down" takes it back up;
- air drag (the US Standard Atmosphere, thinning with height; a drag coefficient that doubles through the sound
  barrier) sets how far things go: your apple (200 g) leaves the window at 3:01:30, is 15.3 km up at 3:06 (−56 °C),
  goes supersonic at 3:07:58 (36 km), crosses 100 km at 3:09:39, coasts up to 211 km after 3:10, and lands at about
  3:21; phones lost at 3:00 reach airliner height (11 km) at 3:07; the fountain's water is 1.6 km up at 3:03:18 and
  5.5 km at 3:10, frozen;
- walking is safe (one foot is always on the ground); running isn't (both feet leave the ground every stride): the
  runner falls into the plane tree on her first stride; if she let go she would reach the edge of space;
- at 3:10 everything comes back: your ceiling lands on your table in 0.61 s (bounces, rolls, the cat on its feet);
  the runner drops to the path; the fountain's top melts on the way down (ice-cold rain until about 3:20).

## How it follows the owner's lessons and playbook

- Everyday place, human scale: your 5th-floor kitchen, the street below, the park across the road.
- Action on frame 1: the glass slips under the title (0.5 s) and hits the ceiling at 2.0 s; a new consequence every
  few seconds after that.
- Real nuance in the first minute: Galileo's rule (with "without air in the way"), and "between masses, gravity only
  ever pulls" at 0:25 (so dark energy isn't a counter-example).
- Not an "X kept getting slower" derivative.
- No clock resets: one forward clock; every cut moves the ten minutes on (the HUD shows "+N s").
- About five minutes (4:58) without a recap; escalation from odd (the glass, the tap, the tea) to funny (the cat, the
  street) to far (the apple, supersonic, space) to dangerous (the runner) to the crash at 3:10.
- Tension to the last frame: the film stops on "Where would you stand?" while the fountain's ice is still coming down
  and your apple is still going up.
- Teaser Shorts: the cat (0:50–1:08), the runner's stride (2:27–2:45), the crash (4:22–4:38).
- Thumbnails (house style, real frames): `thumb-cat.jpg` (10 min, "and the cat can't get down"), `thumb-runner.jpg`
  (5 min 30 s, "or she falls to the edge of space"), `thumb-crash.jpg` (0.6 s, "and the cat comes with it").
