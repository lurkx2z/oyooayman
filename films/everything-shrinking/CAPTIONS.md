[title] What if everything kept shrinking and couldn't stop?

[start] 6:49 AM, a campsite in the Canadian Rockies. Everything except you just started shrinking. / Every 30 seconds it halves. And it isn't going to stop. / The tent is closing in. Your son Leo is asleep right beside you.

[out] You push up through the roof. / The whole campsite is shrinking in towards you.

[rule] In 1908 the physicist Henri Poincaré asked: if everything changed size overnight, would anyone notice? / His answer: no. Every ruler, and every eye, would change with it. / Unless one thing didn't.

[leo] Leo climbs out of the tent. / To him, nothing is shrinking. You're growing.

[voice] He's calling you. Small things ring higher: half the size, an octave up. / His voice is already 2 octaves too high.

[weight] Half the size, half the pull: a smaller Earth holds you less. / You weigh 9 kg. You bend down, and standing back up lifts you off the grass. / You pick him up. He fits in your hand.

[neighbours] The people at the next campsite are screaming. At you. / To them you're 30 metres tall, and growing. / One step could crush them now. You stand still.

[rumble] You call out to them. To them, your voice is far too low to hear. / They feel it come up through the ground, like an earthquake.

[hush] Leo is still calling you. It sounds like a bird now. / Then a whistle. / Then nothing. His voice is too high for you to hear.

[quiet] Every sound the valley makes is now too high for you to hear. / All you can hear is you.

[sunrise] The Sun is coming up. Up here, you see it first. The valley is still in the dark.

[shadow] The peaks across the lake were lit gold. / Now you're blocking their sunrise.

[peaks] The lake is a puddle at your feet. / The mountains around it are 1,500 metres high. They come up to your waist.

[air] To Leo, your hand is now 3 km up. The air there is thin, and 15 degrees below freezing. / You have to put him down. / You kneel, and put your hand down on the meadow. / Down there, he walks off into the grass. / Then you can't see him any more.

[escape] You weigh 14 grams. Jump now, and you'd never come back down. / You hold on to two mountains.

[plane] An airliner, 11 km up, crawls past your face. It's 5 millimetres long.

[curve] The whole Earth is 800 metres across now. You can see it curve away.

[sky] The sky is shrinking too. Its air is all below your eyes. / Above you: black. And stars, at 6:57 in the morning.

[lift] It can barely hold you now. So you hold on to it.

[moon] The Moon is shrinking too, and coming closer. It looks exactly the same size.

[house] It's the size of a house. / Then a car. / Then it's smaller than you.

[ball] It's 30 cm across. Its mass is now 75 kg: the same as yours.

[end] Leo is on it. Everyone is. To them, nothing is shrinking at all. / In 4 minutes, it'll be a grain of sand. In 10, too small for light to show you.

[question] Would you watch it until it's gone?
