[title] What if sound travelled faster than light?

[start] 8:52 PM, on a corn farm in Iowa. Your daughter just ran into the corn after the dog. / And sound just started travelling at 1 million km a second. / The thunder of 9 lightning strikes, still on its way to you, arrived all at once.

[flash] Thunder used to crawl behind the lightning at 343 m/s: 3 seconds for every kilometre. / Now it comes with the flash. / Strictly, it comes first. From 3 km away, 7 millionths of a second before the light.

[rule] Nothing can carry a signal faster than light. Air would have to be 7 million times stiffer than steel. / Here, only sound's speed changes. How loud things are, and how far they carry, stay the same.

[rumble] A lightning bolt is kilometres long. It rumbles because its far end is heard late. / This one is 6 km long. Its thunder used to roll on for 13 seconds. / Now all of it lands at once.

[call] She isn't back. You call her name. The barn should throw it back to you. / No echo. Every echo now arrives with your voice.

[answer] She's calling back. You can hear her perfectly. / You can't tell where she is.

[ears] Your brain finds a sound by the gap between your two ears: up to 0.66 thousandths of a second. / Now the gap is 0.2 billionths. 44,000 times too short to notice. / Her voice's waves are now 3,300 km long. Nothing casts a sound shadow. Not even your head.

[head] Every sound now seems to come from inside your head. / Louder means closer. That's all you have left. / You walk along the corn. She gets quieter.

[far] Distance doesn't make a sound late any more. A freight train blows its horn 11 km away. / You hear it at the same instant as the Hansens' dogs, 1.4 km away. / Everything for kilometres around is happening right now. All of it inside your head.

[bats] The bats are leaving the barn, like every evening. / A bat sees with echoes: 1 thousandth of a second for every 17 cm. Every echo now comes back at once. / Every bat on the night side of Earth is flying blind. So is every dolphin in the sea.

[siren] The tornado sirens in town, 6 km away. You hear them the instant they start. / A sheriff's car. Its siren should drop in pitch as it passes. It doesn't. / You can't tell if it's coming or going.

[tornado] Lit for a fraction of a second: a tornado, 3 km west. / Its roar reaches you the same instant. / It's getting louder. That's all you know.

[phone] You call her phone. It rings as if it were in your own pocket. / She answers. Through the air, her voice now beats the phone to you. / Her phone says she's 120 metres into the corn. / West. Towards the tornado.

[corn] You run into the corn. It's 2.4 metres tall. Your torch shows you one row at a time. / Beyond it, you only see when the lightning strikes. / She sounds like she's right beside you. She's 90 metres away.

[strike] Lightning hits the yard light, 90 metres behind you. / You used to get a quarter of a second between the flash and the bang. / Now the bang is the flash.

[wind] The wind is rushing in towards the tornado at 90 km/h. / The corn is lying down. All of it pointing west.

[neighbour] The Hansen farm, 1.3 km west. The tornado is on it. / You hear it come apart at the very instant it happens.

[found] A flash. A yellow raincoat, 6 metres away. / She's holding the dog.

[back] The storm cellar is 130 metres behind you. The tornado is less than a kilometre away. / You can hear it everywhere. / You can't hear which way it's moving.

[question] Which way would you run?
