[title] What if sound travelled faster than light?

[start] 8:52 PM, a corn farm in Iowa, under a tornado warning. Your daughter just ran into the corn after the dog. / And sound just started travelling at 1 million km a second. / 9 thunderclaps, still crawling across the fields towards you, hit you in the same instant.

[flash] Thunder used to crawl behind the lightning at 343 m/s: 3 seconds for every kilometre. / Now it comes with the flash.

[rule] In real physics this can't happen. Air would have to be 7 million times stiffer than steel.

[rumble] That one was 6 km long. Its thunder used to roll on for 13 seconds, its far end heard last. / Now all of it lands at once.

[call] Maisie isn't back. You call her name. The barn should throw it back to you. / No echo. Every echo now arrives with your voice.

[answer] She's calling back. You can hear her perfectly. / You can't tell where she is.

[ears] Your brain finds a sound by the gap between your two ears: up to 0.66 thousandths of a second. / Now it's 0.2 billionths: 44,000 times too short for your brain. / Her voice's waves are now 3,300 km long. They flow round your head as if it weren't there.

[head] Every sound now seems to come from inside your head. / Louder means closer. That's all you have left. / You walk along the corn. She gets quieter.

[far] A freight train blows its horn 11 km away. / You hear it at the same instant as the Hansens' dogs, 1.4 km away. / Everything for kilometres around, all at once, inside your head. Somewhere in it is her voice.

[siren] The tornado sirens in town, 6 km away. You can't tell which way the town is. / A sheriff's car. Its siren should drop in pitch as it passes. It doesn't. / You can't tell if it's coming or going.

[tornado] In the flash: a tornado, 3 km west. / Its roar is already here. It sounds as close as the rain. / It's getting louder. That's all you know.

[phone] You call her phone again. It rings as if it were in your own pocket. / This time she answers. You hear her twice: through the air, then on the phone. / Her phone says she's 120 metres into the corn. / West. Towards the tornado.

[corn] You run into the corn. It's 2.4 metres tall. Your torch shows you one row at a time. / Beyond it, you only see when the lightning strikes. / She sounds like she's right beside you. She's 90 metres away.

[strike] Lightning hits the yard light behind you.

[wind] The wind is rushing in towards the tornado at 90 km/h. / The corn is lying down. All of it pointing west.

[neighbour] The Hansen farm, 1.3 km west. The tornado is on it. / You hear its roof go. You can't tell if that was a kilometre away, or a hundred metres.

[found] A flash. A yellow raincoat, 6 metres away. / You have her. She's shaking.

[back] The storm cellar is 130 metres behind you. Carrying her, that's 54 seconds. / If the tornado is coming straight at you, its winds reach the cellar in 51. / You can hear it everywhere. You can't hear which way it's moving.

[question] Which way would you run?
