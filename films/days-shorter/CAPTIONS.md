[title] What if days kept getting shorter and couldn't stop?

[start] Sunday, 12:00. From now on, every hour, the day gets 10 minutes shorter. / And it never stops.

[math] Right now a day lasts 24 hours. By Wednesday: 12. By Saturday: about an hour.

[dish] By evening, every satellite dish in town is aimed at empty sky. / The satellites didn't move. You did.

[sunset] 5:52 PM. The sun sets 7 minutes early. / Each sunset will come sooner than the last.

[real] Over the centuries, real days get longer, not shorter: the Moon's tides slow us by 2 milliseconds a century. / Nothing could spin a planet up like this. That's the one impossible part. The rest is physics.

[quake] 11:40 PM. The ground shakes. / The planet is being pulled into a new shape: fatter at the equator. The rock cracks.

[sunrise] Monday. The sun comes up at 4:55 in the morning. / It sets at 3:04 in the afternoon.

[node] On the news: the oceans are moving towards the equator. / North of you, the seas drain and the air thins. South of you, the water piles up. / Your town is 35° north: one of the two latitudes where the sea stays put. For now.

[midnight] Tuesday. The sun comes up at 12:25 in the morning. / Nobody knows when to sleep any more.

[birds] The birds sing at every sunrise. They don't know what day it is either.

[lamps] The street lights come on at every sunset. On Tuesday, that's 9:01 in the morning.

[wed] Wednesday: 12-hour days. Six hours of light, then five and a half of dark.

[scale] A 1 kg bag of sugar now weighs 993 grams. / The spin is flinging everything outwards. Only a little, for now.

[push] And it doesn't push straight up. It pushes away from Earth's axis. / So down isn't straight down any more. It leans towards the equator.

[marble] Today by 0.3°. A marble rolls across your floor. / Always the same way: south.

[quake2] The quakes keep coming. Every day stronger.

[pond] Thursday: 8-hour days. Down leans 0.9°. / The pond slides to its south end.

[car] A car left in neutral, handbrake off, rolls away. / Downhill is south now. Everywhere in town.

[friday] Friday. A sunrise every 4 hours. / Then every 3.

[swings] The swings in the park hang crooked. / So does every plumb line, every lamp, every rope.

[lighter] You're 16 % lighter, and every street feels like a hill. / People lean into it without thinking.

[jump] Kids jump higher than they ever have.

[glass] Water sits crooked in its glass. / The water is level. Your table isn't, any more.

[sat] Saturday, midnight. 2-hour days. Down leans 19°. / At the equator, things already weigh half as much.

[slide] Your sofa slides across the room. / Anything that isn't fixed down is heading south.

[spire] The church spire is being pushed sideways by a third of what it now weighs. / It was never built for that.

[lean] 1:44 AM. Down leans 30°. Standing up is like climbing a roof.

[cars] Even parked cars start to slide sideways.

[equator] 3:33 AM. A day is now 84 minutes long. / At the equator, the ground is moving at orbital speed. Nothing there weighs anything at all.

[sunset2] 3:52 AM. The sun sets in 9 seconds.

[stars] 39 minutes of night. The stars wheel round the Pole Star once every 78 minutes.

[dawn] 4:31 AM. Sunrise number 18. Down leans 76°. / Your street is a cliff now.

[question] At 5:07, down points sideways. And the days are still getting shorter. / What would you hold on to?
