# Episode report — *What if days kept getting shorter and couldn't stop?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 5:35 (334.5 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Friction 10s /
Liquid air formula, built to the owner's YouTube lessons and retention playbook of 28 October 2026 and the rule of 29
October 2026: **the story clock only runs forward**. One clock runs from Sunday 11:12 AM to Saturday 4:32 AM: a
time-lapse where we watch days go by ("TIME-LAPSE ×N" on the HUD), real speed for the human moments ("REAL SPEED"),
and it only jumps forward at a cut ("+N h" on the HUD clock). The film stays on one street and one home all week
(no globe-spinning film, since "Earth spins faster" is taken). It is told by 58 short second-person captions, a
corner readout (LENGTH OF A DAY, SUNRISES SINCE SUNDAY, NEXT SUNSET / SUNRISE), a clock with a DOWN dial from
Wednesday, one context line per scene, labels pinned in the scene, three diagrams, honest tags and the sound. Page
`days-shorter.html`, code `films/days-shorter/`, every number in `PLAN.md` § 2 (computed in `physics.js`), captions
`CAPTIONS.md`.

**The physical reading.** From Sunday 12:00 noon, every hour the length of a day gets 10 minutes shorter, and it
never stops (P = 86,400 − s/6 seconds). **That is the one impossible thing**: nothing could spin a planet up like this,
and real days get longer over the centuries (the Moon's tides, about 2 ms a century; the film says so in the first
minute). The impossible push acts on everything at once. Everything else is what the faster spin itself does:

- the sun races: Sunday's sunset comes 7 min early (5:52 PM), Monday's sunrise at 4:55 AM, Tuesday's at 12:25 AM,
  Wednesday has 6 h of light and 5 h 31 min of dark, Friday a sunrise every 4 hours, then every 3;
- every satellite dish points at empty sky by Sunday evening (the satellite is 1.4° west; a dish beam is ~1.5° wide);
- the rock is pulled into a fatter shape: quakes Sunday 11:40 PM, stronger on Wednesday, trembling from Friday;
- the oceans move towards the equator (+1,000 m there and −2,000 m at the poles by Monday 10 AM, at equilibrium); your
  town is at 35.26° N, where the new level surface crosses the old one, so the sea stays put there (for now);
- the spin's extra outward push: a 1 kg bag of sugar reads 993 g on Wednesday; the push is away from Earth's axis, so
  **down leans south**: 0.3° Wednesday (a marble rolls south), 0.9° Thursday (the pond slides to its south end, a
  car in neutral rolls away), 8.4° Friday 7:30 PM (16 % lighter, streets feel like hills, kids jump higher, water
  sits crooked in its glass), 19° Saturday midnight (the sofa slides), the church spire pushed sideways by 0.35 of
  what it now weighs (it snaps and falls), 30° at 1:44 AM (a roof pitch), parked cars slide at 35°;
- 3:33:40 AM Saturday: a day lasts 84 minutes, the equator moves at orbital speed (7.9 km/s), nothing there weighs
  anything; 3:52 AM: **the sun sets in 9 seconds**; the stars wheel round the Pole Star once every 78 minutes;
- 4:31 AM: sunrise number 18, down leans 76°, **your street is a cliff now**. At 5:07, down points sideways. We end
  on a mother and child at the railings, mid-shot: *What would you hold on to?*

## How it follows the owner's lessons and playbook

- Everyday place, human scale: your flat, your street, the church at its west end, the park to the south.
- Visual action on frame 1: the sun racing across the sky under the title; the first anomaly at 0:18 (the dishes), the
  first early sunset at 0:30.
- Real nuance in the first minute: real days get longer over the centuries (tidal braking); nothing could do this,
  and the HUD names it as the one impossible thing.
- Not an "X kept getting slower" derivative of our own films; not the globe-spinning "Earth spins faster" film.
- No clock resets: one forward clock, jumps only at cuts ("+N h", "+7 s" when the car's second angle skips ahead).
- About six minutes without filler (5:35); escalation from odd (dishes, early sunsets, birds, people out at night) to
  physical (the marble, the pond, the car, the glass) to dangerous (the sofa, the spire, the parked cars, the cliff).
- Tension to the last frame: the last caption plants the next step (5:07, down points sideways) and the film stops
  mid-shot on the question, with no recap and no fade.
- Teaser Shorts: cut from 3:55–4:32 (the sofa and the spire), 4:58–5:06 (the 9-second sunset) and 2:24–2:35 (the
  marble).

## Reviews

Round 1, three independent reviewers on stills every 2 s of the whole first cut (5:45), before the fixes. Scores as
given:

- **Retention** 5/10 overall (hook 3, first 30 s 3, middle 5, payoff 5, clarity 6). Top problems: frame 1 was a calm
  living room; 30 s of explaining before any proof; a run of still explainer shots at 1:20–2:40; Saturday's tilt
  reading as a camera effect; the climax images not landing (no sun disc in the 9-second sunset, a confusing rolled
  "cliff" frame for 20 s).
- **Visuals**: image 6, composition 5, phone readability 6, hero moments 3.5, shots prove their captions 4.5.
  Failing shots: the Moon, the dish, the marble (the camera followed it, so it never moved), the pond, the spire (gone
  in 2 s), the equator, the 9-second sunset, the cliff; the push diagram's text cut off; a ghost mesh in the glass.
  Best: the stars and the Pole Star, people out in daylight at 12:35 AM, the sofa.
- **Physics**: accuracy 8, honesty 7, clarity 8, learning 8, believability 7. Every number checked matched to the
  minute; fourteen wording errors and overstatements (all in PLAN.md § 7), the biggest: "at the equator, a day is 84
  minutes long" (it's 84 minutes everywhere), "Wednesday will last 12" (that's the day's length at that moment), the
  node as "the one latitude" (35° N and S, settled ocean only), the air thinning in your town too.

Fixed after round 1 (the top problems of all three reviewers; details in PLAN.md § 7): the title is outside, on the
sun racing west; the first proof at 0:18 instead of 0:34; the dish seen from behind with its beam drawn out to empty
sky; the marble rolling away from a still camera; the pond's depth gauges; the spire snapping and falling in one wide
frame; the glass rebuilt (no ghost); the 9-second sunset on a long lens with a visible disc and a countdown; a new
sunrise-on-the-cliff ending, closing on a mother and child; an equator diagram; all the physics wording; a Friday
"the air here would be thinning too" tag. No second scoring round was run.

## What is still weak

- The swings are seen end-on, so their 8° lean is hard to see; the birds are specks and the lamps barely glow.
- The HUD is busy (four corner readouts, a dial, a context line, tags).
- Saturday's tilted camera has no plumb line in most frames, so a viewer could still read some of the lean as a
  camera roll; there is no big human-scale jump on Saturday; the Moon is small at that field of view.
- Two honest limits are tags, not consequences: the air (real skies would turn to storms, and by Friday the air in
  your town would be thinning) and the rock (real rock would crack far worse); the lean numbers treat the ground as
  rigid (an elastic Earth would make early leans about 30 % smaller).

## Sound

All synthesised and seeded (`sound-*.js`, `mix.js`), baked into `soundtrack.js` with the shared fingerprint check
(so the ZIP plays its sound outside Chrome too). Places: your room (room tone, the wall clock ticking at real speed),
outdoors (traffic by day, crickets at night, wind rising with the lean). Effects: a chime at every sunrise we see (a
step up the scale each time, coming faster and faster: the film's pulse), TV static and the news murmur, the quakes
(rumble, rattles; car alarms in the second), the scale beep, the marble rolling over the boards, the car rolling into
the railings and its owner running, footsteps, jump thuds, lamps clicking on and humming, furniture scraping into the
window wall (the radiator rings), the spire cracking, creaking, falling and landing, the parked cars' tyres scraping,
the dawn chorus. Score: a D major figure for Sunday, a drone under the quake, an accelerating pulse in B minor through
the week, bells over the jump, a heartbeat on Saturday, hits on the sofa and the spire, a 9-second swell for the last
sunset, stars on F minor 9 with bells, a big chord and the theme at the last sunrise, cut on the question.
Loudness: −14.9 LUFS integrated, −2.3 dBTP true peak, 9.0 LU loudness range in the WAV (the score lifted 3 dB through
the news and the scale/push explainers, which first sat at −23 LUFS); the AAC 256k `final-soundtrack.m4a` for the PC
render, encoded through a limiter because the plain AAC overshot to −0.8 dBTP: −15.3 LUFS, −2.1 dBTP.
