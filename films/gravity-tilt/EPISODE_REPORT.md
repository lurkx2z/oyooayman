# Episode report — *What if gravity tilted 90° for one hour?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 5:18 (317.65 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Friction 10s
formula, built to the owner's YouTube lessons and retention playbook of 28 October 2026 and the rule of 29 October 2026:
**the story clock only runs forward** (no replays; every cut moves the hour on). The film is told by 64 short
second-person captions, a corner readout (GRAVITY BACK IN 59:42, with how fast we're watching under it: REAL SPEED,
SPED UP 25×, and on a jump +8 MIN; after the return GRAVITY · BACK +0:12), a clock (THIS MORNING 7:42:00 AM), a DOWN
dial (which way down is on screen right now), one context line, pinned labels, six small diagrams, and the sound.
Page `gravity-tilt.html`, code `films/gravity-tilt/`, every number in `PLAN.md` § 2 (computed in `physics.js`),
captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The physical reading.** At 7:42:00 on a weekday morning gravity on everything loose turns 90° to point east, along
your street, at its usual strength, for exactly one hour. The air and the ground keep their old down (if the air fell
sideways the wind would pass the speed of sound in about 35 s). Nothing known can turn gravity; the nuance the film
uses is Einstein's: you never feel gravity itself, only what stops your fall, so to you it is as if your house had been
tipped on its side. Then ordinary mechanics:

- you fall 17 cm onto your bedroom window (the mug cracks it); the room falls 0.7–3.4 m onto the window wall; the cat
  lands feet first; everything loose in the street falls the 285 m to the retaining wall at the end and piles up there
  (parked cars arrive at up to 241 km/h: almost no drag over 285 m); the wrecked engines steam, and the steam rises
  the old way (hot air rises because the air around it still weighs the old way);
- window glass is built for about 100 kg/m² of wind: lie flat and it might hold you, kneel and you go through;
- a jogger hooks a lamp post as she falls past it, hangs on past a minute, and climbs on top like a branch;
- a river 4 km west falls along its own bed; where it bends the water doesn't; it breaks into drops that fall east at
  ~34 km/h and arrive as sideways rain at 7:49:26, with a gust; with the sun behind you, a rainbow, right where it
  always is;
- next door's 150-litre bath comes through the edges of your door and lies 1.9 cm deep on your window wall;
- your timber house racks and creeps (wood under a lasting load); one of its 22 anchor bolts snaps at 8:37:30;
- the brick terrace opposite (30 houses, 150 m) is held on its damp-proof course only by the mortar's grip; it creeps,
  passes 30 cm at 8:06, lets go at 8:09:00 and hits the end of the street at 175 km/h;
- your cracked window, carrying the pool and the books under a load that never stops, lets go at 8:24:10 (static
  fatigue): the water pours out as a sideways waterfall;
- at 8:42:00 down is down again: you drop 36 cm, the cat 57 cm, the books fall; the rain still in the air hits the
  ground for about 12 s; your house keeps 0.9° of lean for good.

## How it follows the owner's lessons and playbook

- **Everyday place, bizarre physics:** your bedroom at 7:42 on a weekday, your street, a jogger, a cat, a bath.
- **Action in the first 5 seconds:** down turns at 0:01.5 under the title; you hit the glass at 0:01.7; the room falls
  by 0:02.3; the street is falling by 0:04.
- **The physics nuance in the first minute:** Einstein at 0:41; the air rule at 0:51.
- **No clock-reset replays:** one story clock for the whole film; it only runs forward, and every jump is a cut (checked
  in code).
- **Escalation and tension to the last frame:** the glass you're lying on → the terrace slipping → the rain → the
  terrace goes → your window goes → the bolt → the last ten seconds → the return; no recap; the question is cut hard.
- **5–6 minutes, no filler:** 5:18.

## What is still weak

- The middle (1:26–2:51) is still a run of short explanations (pit, sickness, cat, bath, phone, trees, sun, brick):
  the retention reviewer's biggest worry after the first minute.
- The terrace falls as one block; the reviewers wanted it to break up house by house as it goes.
- The lean of your house (0.9° for good) can't be seen in the picture; only the readout says it.
- The people are the engine's simple figures; the jogger hangs stiffly.
- The opening room shot is busy at 1.75 s long under the title.
- After the return, your room and your house still look fairly tidy: the empty window is hard to spot from the street
  and in the last shot, and the impact is partly hidden by its own dust.

## Sound

All synthesised and seeded (`sound-*.js`, `mix.js`; `js/audio/audioEngine.js` unchanged). Three stems (music, amb,
sfx), a dynamics map per stem, a true-peak limiter.

- the score (D): a calm D major for a second and a half, one low blow, and the chord slides sideways into the **hour
  chord** (no third, a raised fourth); it never lands until 8:42, when it resolves to D major. The theme (its last
  note bent down, like something tipping over) at the title, Einstein, the sun, the rainbow and the question (cut on
  the last frame); a pulse when things move, a heartbeat when a body is at risk; silence before the terrace lets go and
  0.4 s of nothing before the return;
- the places: your room; the street through the walls (and through the hole once the window has gone); the car alarms
  of the pile, as loud as the camera is near it; alarms all over town; the rain's roar coming down the street, a gust,
  then rain everywhere; at 8:42 the rain hitting the ground for 12 s;
- the events at their story seconds, each heard once, scaled and filtered by the camera's distance (and muffled through
  the walls from inside).

Measured (EBU R128): **−15.3 LUFS integrated, loudness range 8.8 LU, true peak −1.4 dBTP** (the mastered WAV); the
final AAC 256k soundtrack (`films/gravity-tilt/final-soundtrack.m4a`) measures −15.4 LUFS and −1.2 dBTP. Target −16 to
−14 LUFS, ≤ −1 dBTP: met.
