# WHAT IF EVERYTHING LOST ITS ELASTICITY? — production plan

A 71.6-second vertical (9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps) first-person film on the shared engine, in the Oxygen/Friction format:
centred serif title over a moving scene, a top-left readout, short serif captions, a new consequence every 5–8 s,
small human things → machines → infrastructure → the payoff. Brief: `docs/briefs/` (Developer 3, 2026-10-07).

**Story clock and the cut.** Camera, script, props, cars and sound all run on one 69.6 s story clock (the times in this
plan are story times). `CONFIG.edit` plays the crash, story 53.35–54.35, at a third of real speed; everything after it
is 2.0 s later on the film clock (film length 71.6 s).

## 1. Rules

| Item | This episode |
|---|---|
| Exact fictional rule | Solids lose **elastic recovery**: they still resist being deformed (stiffness and strength are unchanged), but once deformed they never spring back. Every solid behaves as perfectly plastic. |
| Held constant | Gravity, mass, momentum, air, water, **living tissue** (muscles, tendons, bones, skin behave normally), engines, electricity. **Gases are still springy**: tyres stay round and the bus's air suspension still works. |
| Changed | Rubber, foam, steel springs, strings, fabric webbing, structural steel and concrete: any deformation beyond what they already have becomes permanent. |
| Real consequences (one per beat) | Ball lands and stays flat · racket strings stay stretched · shoe foam stays crushed · cushion keeps the dent · rubber band stays long and slack · trampoline mat stretches into a funnel and stays · soles crushed, legs fine · car springs ratchet lower at each bump · traffic bottoms out, a loaded truck's rear springs flatten and it drags · a footbridge's sag grows with every crossing, a signal arm droops · a collision is perfectly inelastic: the cars crumple and stay locked · the street ends tired and low. |
| Simplifications | A **static** load doesn't sink further (it is below the yield the material already took); only a **larger** or **dynamic** load adds permanent set. Set per spring event is a fixed amount per bump (climb 4.2 cm, land 3.4 cm × load factor), capped at the bump stop. The footbridge sag steps up per crossing group (1 → 7 → 17 → 31 → 48 cm). |
| Misconceptions to avoid | Elasticity is **not** bounciness: it is returning to shape. Rigid things do not turn soft; buildings do not liquefy; engines do not explode; bodies do not go limp. A crash without elasticity is not "safer": nothing springs back, so the cars stay jammed. |
| Formulas / numbers used | Perfectly inelastic collision: one common velocity after contact, momentum and angular momentum conserved (SUV 2000 kg at 34 km/h into a 1400 kg sedan; the van piles in at 54.86). Coefficient of restitution e = 0. Ride height loss shown live (HUD: THE RED CAR SITS n CM LOWER). |
| Must NOT claim | That tyres go flat (the air inside is still springy). That the bus kneels (air suspension). That people collapse. That buildings fall. That this could really happen (the closing note says it is a fictional rule). |

Open comment questions we leave on purpose: mattresses, bones, tyres, watch springs, rubber seals, earthquakes, bridges.

## 2. The place

One sunny avenue (14 m, two lanes each way, kerbs 0.15 m), a raised **speed table** (brick crosswalk, 9 cm) right in
front of you at z −10.4 … −17.6, a junction at z −46 with a signal mast that faces you, a steel **footbridge** at z −86
(deck 6.2 m, 22 m span between two stair towers). On your side (x > 7) a small paved plaza: a half court and hoop, a
round **trampoline** at (19, −12.5), planters, a café front with a cushioned bench at (12.08, 3.2).

You stand on the right-hand sidewalk at (9.6, −0.8), step forward to (8.3, −2.1) for the traffic and stay there until
the end, then turn back to the plaza at (10.4, −2.6).
