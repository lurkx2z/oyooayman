[open] Later tonight, you'll watch light crawl across your living room floor.

[title] What if light kept getting slower, and couldn't stop?

[fast] Light is the fastest thing there is. In one second, it can go around the whole Earth seven times.

[rule] Tonight, it starts slowing down. Just light. Everything else stays the same.

[call] You're on a video call with your gran in Sydney. Your voice and your face cross the world as flashes of light, inside cables under the sea.

[lag] At first, she's a fraction of a second behind. Then a whole second. Then three.

[joke] By the time she laughs at your joke, you've already moved on.

[map] Your map breaks next. It finds you with radio signals from satellites, and radio is light too. Your phone still thinks light is fast. So now it's sure you're in the middle of the sea.

[sun] Outside, night comes early. The Sun hasn't gone anywhere. Its light is just arriving slower, so less of it reaches you.

[fireworks] It's fireworks night. The show is over the harbour, two kilometres away.

[count] You know the trick. See the flash, then count. Six seconds until the bang. Sound is slow.

[gap] But light is getting slower too. Four seconds. Two seconds.

[together] Now they're exactly the same speed. Flash and bang, together.

[first] And now, you hear it before you see it.

[slower] Light is slower than sound.

[street] Out on the street, light is down to motorway speed. About a hundred kilometres an hour.

[blue] Here comes a car. Its headlights look blue. It's driving into its own light, squashing the waves together, like a siren that sounds higher as it comes towards you.

[red] As it drives away, its brake lights turn deep red. Then they vanish. Stretched into infrared, a colour your eyes can't see.

[faster] Light keeps slowing. And the next car is faster than light.

[cantsee] You can hear it coming. But you can't see it. It's ahead of its own light.

[two] Then it appears out of nowhere, and splits in two. One car drives on, past you. The other drives backwards, the way it came.

[past] They're the same car. The backwards one is its past, arriving late, in the wrong order.

[cat] Back inside. Light is now slower than your cat.

[laser] So you find the laser pointer. Point it at the wall. And wait.

[dot] Your cat isn't watching the dot. She's watching your hand. And she gets there first.

[beat] Your cat just beat light.

[mirror] In the mirror by the door, light is down to walking pace. Wave at yourself, and wait for it.

[late] There you are. Two seconds late.

[walk] Walk towards the mirror, and everything ahead of you turns blue. You're walking into the light, so its waves hit you faster.

[behind] Look back, and it's dark and red. That light can hardly catch up with you.

[lamp] In the living room, light is slower than you walk. Switch off the lamp.

[stays] The room stays bright. The dark spreads out from the lamp, slowly, like spilt ink.

[on] Switch it back on. This is what you saw at the start. Light, crawling across the floor.

[egg] It reaches you in an egg shape. Every bit of light has to get from the lamp to the floor, and then from the floor to you.

[knock] Light is slower than a tortoise now. Knock on the table.

[hear] You hear the knock right away. You'll see your hand land in a few seconds.

[see] There.

[freeze] Nothing new is reaching you any more. Everything in front of you is a picture of a moment that's already over. And it's fading.

[stop] Then light stops.

[last] The last light you'll ever see is a millimetre from your eye. It isn't coming.

[door] In the dark, you hear the front door. It's your mum. She asks why all the lights are off.

[on2] They're not. Every light in the house is on.

[text] Somewhere between the phone mast and your phone, her text saying she's on her way is still travelling. She beat it home.

[real] Here's the strangest part. Some of this has really happened.

[lab] In nineteen ninety-nine, scientists at Harvard sent light through a cloud of atoms, cooled to almost absolute zero. And it slowed down to seventeen metres a second. About as fast as a car driving through town.

[stopped] Two years later, they stopped light completely. Held it, for a split second. And let it go again.

[question] So, if light slowed down to walking speed, what's the first thing you'd race?
