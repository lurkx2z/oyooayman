# Episode report — *What if the universe kept expanding faster and faster?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 5:56 (356.1 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light film's format (the
owner's call on 2026-10-25: "you know exact formula of the slower light works"): the film is told by 85 short
second-person captions, a corner readout and the sound. One quantity keeps getting worse and can't stop: **how long
space takes to double**. Page `universe-faster.html`, code `films/universe-faster/`, every number in `PLAN.md` (from
`physics.js`), captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The idea.** Tonight at 7:30 the growth of space starts speeding up, and it never stops. You are in your back garden
with a telescope, a cup of tea, a candle in a lantern and your cat. The night runs to midnight, when space itself
tears (a "Big Rip", the textbook phantom dark energy case with w = −1.5). Then: what's real (the acceleration,
measured since 1998), what probably isn't (a rip), and the real, slower future.

## Reviews (independent, first cut) and what changed

**Viewer review:** enjoyment **6/10**, retention **5/10**, clarity **5/10**, visuals **5/10**. Strongest: the cat
frozen mid-leap; every star over the edge and nothing looks different; the last second. **Science review:** science
accuracy **8/10**, clarity of the science **7/10**. It recomputed every on-screen number (H0 = 67.9 km/s/Mpc, the
edge crossings, weight, the fountain, the jump, the boiling point, Earth's layers, the stretch, the last second) and
found them right; the problems were wording and two display bugs.

The fixes below were made after those reviews and have **not been re-scored**.

Science fixes:
1. *"Speeding up" meant two things.* The film's rule (the doubling time shrinks) is not what was measured in 1998
   (galaxies recede faster, but the real doubling time is slowly getting longer). Now: "At 7:30, the doubling time
   starts to shrink."; at the end "The speeding up is. Galaxies leave faster every year." … "But the doubling time
   isn't shrinking."; the readout says AND SLOWLY GETTING LONGER.
2. *"The news is still on its way" / "Now the stretch reaches you"* implied something travels to deliver the change.
   Now "Space has hardly grown yet." and "Now the stretch starts to show."
3. *The last-second clock* read "0.1.0 78 s" above 1 s. Fixed ("1.078 s").
4. *The edge label* said "PAST IT, SPACE GROWS FASTER THAN LIGHT"; now "THINGS MOVE AWAY FASTER THAN LIGHT".
5. *Letting go* read as a sequence; they all go at 7:30 at once. New caption "All at once." and the chart says so.
6. Smaller: "Every galaxy drifts away" (Andromeda is coming towards us) is cut; "Andromeda's last light goes out" →
   "Andromeda fades out" (its light is stretched out of sight, not used up); the candle "goes round" → "grows rounder"
   (at 20–30% weight hot air still rises, more slowly); 0.47 atm is LIKE THE TOP OF KILIMANJARO, not Everest; THE
   MANTLE → THE UPPER MANTLE; after the garden drifts free the readout no longer says you're pulled up (you float off
   with it); NOT GROWING → NOT GROWING NOW; "about 100 billion years"; PLAN now says ~170 g across you (head to foot,
   as the readout computes).

Viewer fixes:
1. *The dark middle* (1:55–3:05, stars too faint, labels pointing at nothing): brighter, bigger stars, a STARS YOU CAN
   SEE count that falls 976 → 794 → 459 → 109 → 18 → 0, and label rings that sit clear of the star they point at.
2. *The crack you couldn't see*: a glowing line now runs across the lawn, with its light spilling up.
3. *The opening dragged*: one caption cut, two merged (the film is 5:56).
4. *Text clutter in the first second*: the edge readout hides while the chart is up, the chart's legend is shorter,
   and there is a second more empty screen before each of its beats.
5. *"The edge" never explained, and the sphere never shrank*: the caption now says "Past the edge, space carries them
   off faster than light."; the camera pulls in slower than the edge closes, so it shrinks about 3× on screen.
6. *Captions before the picture*: the Moon has gone out by the end of "The Moon goes out."; the jump happens just
   before "You hang in the air.", looking down as the lawn drops away; "Everything that isn't fixed down… starts to
   fall up" now shows the chairs and your mug rising past the lit kitchen window.
7. *The clock said 12:00:00 PM before midnight*: it holds 11:59:59 until midnight.
8. The real universe at the end now grows visibly (it was nearly still).

**What feels recycled (own review).** The galaxy field is used three times (space grows, what's real, the future);
the Moon in the long lens comes back three times (on purpose: it is the last thing left); the wide garden view is the
main set; "it's gone but you can still see it" is the idea of Andromeda, the stars, Saturn and the Sun (it is the
film's central idea, but the fourth time is the weakest); the red-lit house appears in the cold open, adrift, in
pieces and in the last second; "lets go" is said of the galaxy, Earth's layers and the last second.

## Sound

Score, ambience and effects are all synthesised (`sound-music.js`, `sound-amb.js`, `sound-sfx.js`, levels in
`mix.js`). A score in D minor whose theme ends on an octave (a doubled frequency); the sky's chord slides down as
starlight stretches; in the last second, one tick for every doubling of space. The garden at night (the town, a
breeze, the bird bath, crickets, an owl, a train) gets quieter and duller as the air thins, and from 11:36 there is
only what is inside you: your breathing and your heart. In the two slowed-down seconds the garden's sound is slowed
with the picture into a low throb; the cat's landing, sixty-odd times slower, is a long low thud. Your ears popping
dulls everything for a moment.

Measured on the final soundtrack (`final-soundtrack.m4a`, AAC 256k, what the 1080p file carries): **−15.7 LUFS**
integrated, loudness range **7.1 LU**, true peak **−1.4 dBTP**. The small copy's 128k soundtrack goes through a peak
limiter first (low-bitrate AAC overshoots): −15.8 LUFS, 7.0 LU, −2.2 dBTP. The mix has a gentle tilt (−2.5 dB below
110 Hz, +2 dB above 3.5 kHz) so phones and laptops hear the piano and the garden. `soundtrack.js` is baked from the
same render, so the page plays it the moment it opens.

## Still weak

- "You" are never seen: no hands or body (the jump is felt through the camera only).
- The far-away science (the edge, the stretch) is carried by illustrations and diagrams; younger viewers may still
  lose the idea that light already on its way keeps arriving.
- The Moon shots are a small disc in a black frame; "The Moon goes out" is a fade in an otherwise empty shot.
- The fixes have not been re-reviewed; the scores above are for the first cut.
- The mix was checked by measurement (loudness, true peak, band levels, a spectrogram), not by watching the whole
  film with sound.
