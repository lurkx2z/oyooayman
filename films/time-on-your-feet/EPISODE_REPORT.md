# Episode report — *Is it true time moves slower on your feet than your head?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 7:43 (463 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Universe shrinking format
(the owner's brief on 2026-10-25: that formula, accurate science, about 8 minutes with no filler). The film is told by
116 short second-person captions, a corner readout and the sound. One quantity keeps growing: **how much younger your
feet stay than your head over an 80-year life**, from 0.000 000 47 s at home to 21 hours on a neutron star and then
without limit at a black hole's edge. Page `time-on-your-feet.html`, code `films/time-on-your-feet/`, every number in
`PLAN.md` (computed in `physics.js`), captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The idea.** Yes, it's true. Two of the most precise clocks ever built stand on a pole in your hallway, one at your
feet and one at your head, and you watch them drift apart. Then: the five experiments that measured it, the GPS in your
pocket and the Moon's own time, why it happens (on Earth, slower time at your feet *is* gravity), and three places where
the gap grows until, on a platform above Sagittarius A*'s edge, your head ages a year for every day at your feet and
then your feet freeze. Then: is any of this real, home again, and a question for the comments.

## Sound

Synthesised, three stems. An original E minor score whose main idea is the physics: the two clocks are one eight-note
figure in two identical voices (head right, feet left), each played at its own clock's rate. They are in unison
wherever the gap is too small to hear; above the black hole the feet's voice slips behind (×1.17), plays at half speed
(×2), one note in eleven, all but stops, and finally hangs on one note; from your feet's view the head's voice races.
The neutron star's sky chord slides up ×1.235 (365 cents); the black hole floor's glow slides down and out of hearing.
Ambience for each place (the house at 7 a.m., the labs, the tower shaft, the airliner, the wind at 450 m, space, the
spacesuit, the glowing ground, the platform's deep pressure that thins at the edge) and effects on the picture (the
clocks' beep, the ball's bounces, the platform's motor and stops, the toes' creak at half speed, the freeze).

**Loudness (measured, ffmpeg ebur128):** mix −14.7 LUFS integrated, LRA 6.7 LU, true peak −1.3 dBTP.
`final-soundtrack.m4a` (AAC 256k): −14.8 LUFS, −1.1 dBTP. `small-soundtrack.m4a` (AAC 128k, limited first because
low-bitrate AAC added peaks up to −0.3 dBTP): −15.3 LUFS, −2.7 dBTP. The page plays the baked copy (`soundtrack.js`)
at once. A cloud render of this soundtrack takes about 25 minutes.
