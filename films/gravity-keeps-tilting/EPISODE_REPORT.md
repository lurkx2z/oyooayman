# Episode report — *What if gravity kept tilting sideways every second and couldn't stop?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 6:15 (375.4 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Friction 10s
formula, built to the owner's YouTube lessons and retention playbook of 28 October 2026 and the rule of 29 October
2026 at its strictest: **the story clock runs at real speed from the first frame to the last**. Down turns one degree
a second, so the film's six minutes are one full turn, and every cut lands on the next moment of the same turn. The
film is told by 58 short second-person captions, a corner readout (DOWN HAS TURNED 47°, +1° EVERY SECOND, and one
context line: the spire's lean, the fastest car so far, the park's slope, the water left in the pond, the cars'
height, the count to 360°, the first car's landing), a clock (SATURDAY 4:00:47 PM), a DOWN dial (which way down is
on screen right now; AWAY / AT YOU when it points along the view), pinned labels, three diagrams, and the sound.
Page `gravity-keeps-tilting.html`, code `films/gravity-keeps-tilting/`, every number in `PLAN.md` § 2 (computed in
`physics.js` and a rigid-body simulation baked once offline, `bake-sim.cjs` → `simdata.js`), captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The physical reading.** At 4:00:00 on a Saturday in late October down starts to turn east (your right, looking out
of your window at the park), one degree on each second, at its usual strength: east at 90°, straight up at 180°, west
at 270°, down again at 360°, and on. The air and the ground keep their old down. Nothing known can turn gravity; the
nuance the film uses is your inner ear (the otoliths feel the tilt while your eyes see a level room: seasickness).
Then ordinary mechanics:

- a football on your floor rolls off on the first tick (its rolling resistance is about a third of sin 1°); the pond,
  level to a few centimetres, spills over its east bank at once;
- a slender stone spire standing on its weight alone tips at ~10°, visibly at 13°, and is off the tower by 16° (the
  tower, concrete with steel inside, holds); the books pour off the shelves at ~14°;
- friction lets go at ~25°: your furniture avalanches into the east wall; the parked cars slide down the street and
  pile into the church at nearly 80 km/h; a mother and her daughter get into the playground's climbing dome;
- at 90° everything loose starts to fall into the sky; it falls at its terminal speed in still air, so as down turns
  it goes round a circle of radius speed × 57 s (leaves ~90 m, raindrops ~500 m, cars ~5 km); the stack of cars
  topples off the church wall, the pond rains up, roof tiles peel off their battens, the weakest balconies hinge up
  and tear off (most of a balcony's steel is at the top);
- at 180° you stand on your ceiling; your block hangs from its foundations (only the steel in its columns holds); the
  ceiling slab under you, built to carry weight down, cracks; the furniture crosses the ceiling and smashes the lamp;
- at 270° everything up there is as high as it goes (the cars about 10 km); then it comes back down, mostly
  kilometres west of where it left;
- at 4:06:00 down is down for exactly one second; the last tiles have already landed; the first car is due at
  4:07:05, 6 km west, slanting in at over 300 km/h, and down keeps turning.

## How it follows the owner's lessons and playbook

- **Everyday place, bizarre physics:** your living room on a Saturday afternoon, the park across the street, a
  football, a mother and daughter, parked cars, a church, pigeons, swings.
- **Action in the first 5 seconds:** the degree counter ticks from frame 1 and the football rolls off under the title.
- **The physics nuance in the first minute:** the spire's balance at 0:10, the inner ear at 0:20, friction at 0:28.
- **No clock-reset replays:** one clock at real speed; no cut moves time.
- **Escalation and tension to the last frame:** the room, the street, the sky, the ceiling, as high as it goes, the
  count, one second of normal, and a live countdown to the first car landing under the last question; no recap.
- **5–6 minutes, no filler:** 6:15 (one full turn is 6:00; the extra is the title and the last question).

the film). Scores as given:

Their points and what changed are in `PLAN.md` § 7. In short: the second half was ~60–75 s of one white plume on blue
sky; the first seconds had nothing moving; the hero moments were too small or hidden; and the ending's claim ("it all
comes back past where it left, sideways") was wrong by the film's own model. All of that was reworked (see § 7).
The second cut was checked by me on a new preview and contact sheets; it was not re-reviewed independently, so there
are no new scores.

**Second look (the 6:15 cut).** A new 480×270 preview with the new soundtrack and contact sheets at one frame every
2 s, checked by me (not an independent re-review, so no new scores). The new moments read: the football rolling off
under the title, the pond spilling with the mother and daughter labelled, the spire followed down, the cars sliding
past you, the two of them close on the dome's bars at 85°, the roof tiles peeling, the balconies hinging up and tearing
off, the long lens on the car 8 km up, the girl hand over hand at 247°, the big count, you standing for the one second,
and the countdown to the first car landing. Fixed on that pass: the rain going up (the camera stood against a house)
and the trees (a blob of crown against the sky).

## What is still weak

- 90° (1:30–1:37) is a low, pale shot of the water and leaves starting to leave the park; it marks the moment but is
  not a hero image.
- The pond's drops are still the biggest thing in the sky, so a white plume recurs in several shots from 4:16 to
  6:12 (each now has the tower, a block, the dome or roofs in frame, and none runs much past 6 s).
- The circle (1:55–2:04) is shown by the diagram over a hazy aerial, not drawn in the world.
- The pigeon flying belly-up is clear in the frames but small on a phone.
- The church tower holds because it is concrete with steel inside (said by a label), not shown being tested.
- Thinner air high up isn't modelled (the cars would go a little higher and faster); the film says "about 10 km".
- The preview was rendered at 480×270; the final 1080p needs the PC render.

## Sound

All synthesised and seeded (`sound-*.js`, `mix.js`; `js/audio/audioEngine.js` unchanged). Three stems (music, amb,
sfx), a dynamics map per stem, a true-peak limiter.

- the score (A, 60 bpm, so a soft piano note can land on the ticks): one key per quarter turn (A minor on the ground,
  A lydian floating up, D minor upside down, the high lydian at 270°, warm F and C coming back down), a blow on the
  low drum every 90°; the theme (A–E–D–C, falling, like something tipping) at the title, the circle, the girl and the
  question; A major, wide open, for the one second at 360°, then it slides a semitone down and the film ends on the
  question, cut on the last frame;
- the tick: every second, as down jumps 1°, a soft knock through the building;
- the places: your room's tone, the park through the window, a Saturday town that goes quiet; the pond spilling; the
  car alarms of the pile, each set off as its car hits and carried up into the sky with it; alarms all over town;
- the events at their story seconds, each heard once, by material and speed, as loud as the camera is near them and
  muffled through the walls from inside (the football, the furniture, the spire, the cars into the church, the
  balconies tearing, the ceiling lamp, the crack).

Measured (EBU R128): **−15.3 LUFS integrated, loudness range 5.9 LU, true peak −1.4 dBTP** (the mastered WAV and the
final AAC 256k soundtrack `final-soundtrack.m4a`). Target −16 to −14 LUFS, ≤ −1 dBTP: met. The loudness range is
narrower than film 1's (8.8 LU): the ticks and the alarms keep a floor under the quiet passages.
