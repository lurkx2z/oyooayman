[open] 3 PM today. The high street. / Every footstep is a sonic boom. / And by midnight, it gets much stranger.

[title] What if sound kept getting slower and couldn't stop?

[seven] 7:00 AM. Saturday. Your back garden. / Sound crosses it at 343 metres a second. / From now on, that speed halves. Every hour.

[bird] A blackbird sings on the fence. / Its song reaches you a sixtieth of a second late. / Nobody would ever notice. Yet.

[plane] 7:14. An airliner, 11 kilometres up. / Up there, sound is now slower than the plane. / It's breaking the sound barrier.

[boom] 7:27. Down here, sound is finally slower too. / The boom reaches the ground. / And not just from this plane.

[carpet] A sonic boom isn't one bang. / It's a cone each plane drags across the whole country.

[eight] 8:00. 171.5 metres a second. Half. / Your neighbour says good morning. / His voice sounds strangely deep.

[throat] His voice box buzzes at the same pitch. / But the air in his throat rings an octave lower. / It's what the deep-voice gas, sulphur hexafluoride, does.

[kettle] Inside, the kettle's whistle sinks too. / A whistle is just a little pocket of ringing air.

[radio] The radio sounds perfectly normal. / That song was recorded before today. / Strings and speakers don't care. Only air does.

[nine] 9:00. 86 metres a second. A bullet train. / Every echo in the kitchen now lasts 4 times longer.

[spoon] Drop a spoon, / and your kitchen rings like a church.

[ten] 10:00. 43 metres a second. / Voices sink below their own vowels. / Words stop sounding like words. / So everyone starts texting.

[motorway] 10:27. The motorway, two miles away, starts to crackle. / Every car on it is now faster than sound.

[eleven] 11:00. 21 metres a second. / You walk to the shops.

[siren] An ambulance comes towards you. / Its siren screams 2.6 times higher. / Then it passes, and groans.

[silent] 11:41. 13 metres a second. / Now every car in town is faster than sound. / You can't hear them coming.

[after] You only hear a car once it's passed. / A crack. Then its engine, from both directions at once.

[backwards] This driver has his music on loud. / It reaches you backwards. / The last notes he played arrive first.

[noon] 12:00. 10.7 metres a second. / The church bell rings noon, 800 metres away. / You hear it at 12:01.

[ball] And every hard kick / cracks like a whip. / A whip's crack is a tiny sonic boom.

[flying] Even sound that's already flying / slows down with everything else.

[horizon] So the noon bell can never be heard / more than 56 kilometres away. Ever.

[one] 1:00 PM. 5.4 metres a second. / A moderate breeze is now faster than sound. / Your friend shouts from downwind. / Nothing ever reaches you.

[two] 2:00 PM. 2.7 metres a second. Jogging speed. / Sound stops bending round corners. / Step behind a van, and the street goes quiet.

[ears] Every sound now reaches one ear, / then, almost a tenth of a second later, the other.

[three] 3:00 PM. 1.3 metres a second. / Walking pace. / Every step breaks the sound barrier.

[popcorn] The whole high street / crackles like popcorn.

[four] 4:00 PM. Home. 67 centimetres a second. / The TV is badly dubbed. / Its sound takes 4.5 seconds to cross the kitchen.

[clap] Clap once, / and the room will ring for more than 4 minutes.

[earbuds] The last normal sound / is in your earbuds, a centimetre from your eardrum.

[five] 5:00 PM. 33 centimetres a second. / Your cat now walks faster than her own meow. / She arrives first. Then the meow.

[hum] Your voice is just a hum now. / The air in your mouth rings too low for anyone to hear.

[storm] 6:00 PM. A storm rolls in. / Lightning, one kilometre away. / You will never hear this thunder. / From now on, no sound can travel more than 870 metres.

[eight2] 8:00 PM. 4 centimetres a second. / Slower than a tortoise. / Every sound made today is still out there, crawling.

[box] 10:00 PM. 1 centimetre a second. / You wind up a music box, 3 metres away. / It plays its whole tune. / You hear nothing.

[walkinto] Walk into its tune, / and its notes hit you over 100 times too fast. / Too high to hear.

[wait] So you sit still. / And wait.

[arrives] 10:05 PM. / Here it comes. / A little lower and slower than it left.

[midnight] Midnight. 2.6 millimetres a second. / About as fast as a champion racing snail. / Nothing you say now can ever reach further than 14 metres.

[stop] And it doesn't stop. / 3 AM: 1.7 metres. / 6 AM: 21 centimetres. Not even to the pillow next to you.

[last] The world goes silent. / Except one sound. / Your heartbeat. It travels through you, not through the air.

[real] Is any of this real?

[real2] Sound really does change speed. / On Mars, it's about 240 metres a second. / And high notes travel faster than low ones.

[real3] In sulphur hexafluoride, about 130. / In bubbly water, it can drop to about 20. / That's why a fresh mug of hot chocolate clinks lower.

[real4] And in a cloud of atoms near absolute zero, / scientists watched sound crawl at a few millimetres a second.

[real5] But air can't do this. / To slow sound to half its speed, / air would have to be colder than liquid nitrogen.

[question] So what's the first sound you'd miss?
