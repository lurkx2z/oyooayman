# Research: the North Tower on September 11, 2001

This film is a memorial. The person you play is a **composite**: no real person, no real company. The times, places,
numbers and conditions on screen come from the sources listed at the end. Where sources differ, both are noted and the
film uses the 9/11 Commission's times.

## 2 · The towers

- **Height:** North Tower roof 1,368 ft (417 m), South Tower 1,362 ft; the North Tower carried a 360 ft antenna.
  110 stories above the plaza, seven levels below (FEMA 403 ch. 2).
- **Floor plate:** a square about 207–208 ft (63 m) a side with chamfered corners, "about an acre" per floor.
- **Core:** about 87 × 135 ft (26.5 × 41 m) in the middle. In the North Tower its long side runs **east–west** (in the
  South Tower, north–south). It held **99 passenger elevators** (23 of them express), 7 freight elevators and the
  **three stairways**.
- **Office floor:** column-free. Floor trusses span about **60 ft** (18 m) from the core to the north and south walls and
  about **35 ft** (11 m) at the east and west ends. 4 in of lightweight concrete on a steel deck.
- **Outer wall:** 59 narrow box columns per face, 14 in square, **3 ft 4 in apart** (just over a yard), with **22 in of
  glass** between them. Spandrel plates 52 in deep. The windows were barely wider than a person's shoulders: the
  architect, Minoru Yamasaki, was known to be afraid of heights.
- **Elevators and sky lobbies:** express elevators (55 passengers, 1,600 ft per minute) ran from the lobby to **sky
  lobbies on floors 44 and 78**, where people changed to local elevators.
- **Stairways:** three, in the core. **A and C are 44 in wide, B is 56 in wide.** They were not straight shafts all the
  way down: at **transfer hallways** (FEMA names levels including the 42nd, 48th, 76th and 82nd floors) a stairway
  ended and continued a short walk away. NIST reports occupants hesitating at these hallways.
- **After the 1993 bombing** (six killed, a smoky four-hour evacuation in the dark) the Port Authority added
  battery-backed emergency lighting and **photoluminescent (glow-in-the-dark) markings** in the stairways. NIST lists
  the glowing markings among the most common aids survivors named.
- **Sprinklers:** retrofitted around 1990; nearly 100 % coverage by 2001.
- **Exit route:** the North Tower's stairways A and C came out at the **mezzanine** (plaza level), above the West
  Street lobby. Port Authority and NYPD officers sent people down a (stopped) **escalator into the concourse**, the
  underground shopping mall under the plaza, and out **to the north and east**, away from the debris falling on the
  plaza, West Street and Liberty Street. Officers at the stairs by **5 WTC** urged people on **Vesey Street** to "keep
  walking north" (Commission ch. 9).

## 3 · Inside the North Tower, below the impact

- **People:** NIST estimates **8,900 ± 750** people in the North Tower at 8:46 and 8,540 in the South Tower (17,400
  in all). About **7,500** got out of the North Tower.
- **Above the impact:** all three stairways were destroyed from floor 92 up. **No one above the 91st floor of the North
  Tower survived.** The film never shows those floors; it only says so.
- **What people felt:** NIST's survey of North Tower survivors: 63 % first *felt* something, 30 % *heard* it, 2 % saw
  the plane. Survivors describe the tower **swaying** (some feet at the top), ceiling tiles falling, lights flickering,
  the smell of jet fuel (kerosene). The fireball went down elevator shafts as far as the 77th and 22nd floors, the
  West Street lobby and level B4 (Commission).
- **No announcements:** the North Tower's PA was attempted but almost nobody heard it: the impact damaged the
  communications closet on the 22nd floor (NIST). People decided for themselves.
- **When they left:** median delay before starting down was **3 minutes** for floors below 77 (NIST). 92 % of North
  Tower survivors had started before the second plane hit; 21 % were already out by 9:03.
- **On the stairs:** the most common obstacle was **crowding** (73 % in the North Tower), then **firefighters coming
  up**, lack of information, and injured or disabled people. The most common help: **each other**, first responders
  and the glowing markings. 51 % of survivors had never used a WTC stairway before. Typical descent was around
  **one floor a minute** with stops (NIST stairwell analysis: about 1.2 floors per minute while moving).
- **The second impact, felt from the North Tower stairs:** "The building shook. Not anything like the first time
  [but] it rocked. We stopped briefly on the 31st floor." (a survivor from the 60s, North Tower, quoted by NIST).
- **Conditions lower down:** below the impact the stairways were "intact, lit, and clear of smoke" when firefighters
  started up; stairwell B was "clear and bright" from the upper 80s down (Commission). Water from broken pipes and
  sprinklers ran down the stairs on the lower floors (widely reported by survivors; the concourse sprinklers were
  running).
- **Firefighters:** climbed in full gear (black FDNY bunker coats with yellow reflective stripes, helmets, air tanks,
  hoses and tools; often quoted as 60–100 lb), stopping to rest every few floors. By about 10:00 one engine company had
  reached the 54th floor (Commission). Evacuees moved to one side to let them up, and to let the injured down.
- **Courage on the stairs:** at least two Port Authority staff (Frank De Martini, Pablo Ortiz) climbed up instead of
  down, freeing people trapped behind jammed doors; on the 27th floor, Abe Zelmanowitz stayed with his friend Ed Beyea,
  who used a wheelchair. None of them survived. The film does not portray real victims; it honours them on the card.
- **The South Tower's collapse, felt inside the North Tower:** the building shook, debris and dust came up the
  stairs, the lobby windows broke, power failed and emergency lights came on; many thought it was a bomb or a partial
  collapse (Commission; Chief Hayden). The North Tower's own evacuation rate fell to about a fifth of its peak.

## 4 · The losses

- **2,977** people were killed on September 11, 2001: **2,753 in New York**, 184 at the Pentagon, 40 on Flight 93
  (9/11 Memorial & Museum).
- **441 first responders**, among them **343 FDNY firefighters**, 37 Port Authority police officers and 23 NYPD
  officers.
- About 95 % of the civilians who died in the towers worked at or above the impact zones; 110 worked below them
  (Commission). The evacuation below the impact was, in the Commission's words, "a success": under one hour for most,
  against more than four hours in 1993.
- The Memorial has **2,983 names**: the 2,977 of 2001 and the six killed in the bombing of February 26, 1993.
- Since 2002, on each anniversary, two beams of light (the **Tribute in Light**) rise over Lower Manhattan: 88
  searchlights in two 48-ft squares, set up in recent years on the roof of the Battery Parking Garage, a few
  blocks south of the site, so the beams echo the towers without standing exactly where they stood.

## 5 · The morning

Clear and cool, about 20 °C, an almost cloudless deep-blue sky ("severe clear", as pilots call it); a light breeze from
the north-west carried the smoke south-east over Brooklyn. Primary election day in New York.

## 6 · What the film shows, and what it doesn't

- **Shown:** an office floor in 2001 (CRT monitors, beige towers, cubicles, ceiling tiles); the tower swaying; paper
  and smoke outside the windows; the stairway (44 in wide, two abreast, glowing step edges, floor numbers, the
  transfer hallway); firefighters climbing; water on the stairs; the mezzanine and the concourse; Vesey Street; the
  dust cloud at 9:59; the North Tower from a distance at 10:28; the Tribute in Light; the remembrance card.
- **Never shown:** the planes, the impacts from outside, anyone falling or jumping, injuries in detail, bodies,
  victims' faces or names, real companies. Events are told by sound, light, dust, smoke and the captions.
