[title] What if there was no light in the universe?

[start] 2:40 PM, on a frozen lake in Norway. Every light in the universe just went out. / Not a power cut. The Sun is still in the sky. / Your eyes are wide open. There's nothing left for them to catch.

[skaters] You're still gliding at 20 km/h on two thin blades. So is everyone around you. / And nobody can see.

[heat] But everything still glows, in a light no eye can see: infrared. / This is what a heat camera would see.

[rule] Light is one kind of electromagnetism. Take all of it away, and the force that holds your atoms together goes too. / You wouldn't be in the dark. You'd come apart. / Here, only the light eyes use is gone: 380 to 750 nanometres.

[body] Your skin glows brightest at 9.5 micrometres: a wave 13 times too long for any eye. / Where your hand touched the ice, the ice is still glowing.

[count] 240 people were skating here. Every one of them is out on the ice, in the dark. / It's −12 °C. Nobody knows which way the shore is.

[grab] Someone just grabbed your arm. / She was skating behind you. She can't see you either.

[help] She's calling for help by feel: five presses on the side button. So is everyone else in Norway. / Every ambulance driver is blind too. Nobody is coming.

[sun] The Sun is less than 3° above the hills. You can still feel it on your face. / It sets at 3:29 PM. Nobody will see it.

[fire] The bonfire by the kiosk is still burning at 800 °C. It makes no light at all. / A man is walking straight towards it. / He feels it a metre away, and stops. Fire you can't see is still fire.

[world] Right now, 8 billion people are blind. / Every plant on Earth just stopped making food. They live on exactly this light. / And every plane in the sky is still flying.

[plane] This one is 12 km from its runway, 630 metres up. / Its pilots can't read a single instrument.

[road] On the shore road, every car stopped where its driver went blind. You head for their engines. / One lorry is still creeping along, by feel. / Sound takes 1.5 seconds to cross the ice to you.

[slide] It shoved a stopped car through the railing. / It's out on the lake now. Its driver is still inside.

[ice] Clear ice 5 cm thick can hold a person. A car needs about 20. / Where the stream comes in, the ice is only 8. / It's going through.

[steam] Lake water under ice is at 0 °C. That's 12 degrees warmer than the air. / To a heat camera, the hole glows. To everyone out here, it's a trap they can't see.

[sing] Lake ice cracks as it cools, and the sound runs through it for kilometres. / In the dark, sound is all you have.

[water] Someone is in the water. / The driver got out as it sank. He's holding on to the edge of the ice. / In water at 0 °C, you have 1 minute to get your breath back. About 10 before your arms stop working.

[crawl] Lying flat spreads your weight over your whole body, not two thin blades. / She's holding your ankles. You crawl towards his voice. / He's 40 metres away.

[edge] The ice cracks under your hands. / Cracking isn't breaking. Not yet. / He's been in the water for 9 minutes. You're 3 metres from the edge.

[reach] You reach out. Your fingers find his sleeve. / And the ice under your chest starts to bend.

[question] Would you let go?
