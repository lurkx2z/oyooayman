[title] What if your car could fly? Tesla says this one can.

[ramp] Here's what the new Roadster actually did.

[how] It drove upside down, then flew off the end. So how does a car hang from the ceiling, when a plane can't?

[three] There are only three ways to beat gravity. The first is a wing.

[lift] A wing pushes air down as it moves, and the air pushes back up. That's lift.

[inverted] And yes, planes can fly upside down. Stunt planes just tip the nose up, so the wing keeps pushing air down.

[stall] But lift needs speed. Slow down, and the wing has nothing to push against. A wing can't just hang there.

[suction] The Roadster uses the second way. Suction.

[fans] Fans pull the air out from under the car, so the air around it presses harder than the air beneath. Tesla says that's up to two g's of pull.

[tenth] And it doesn't take much. Less than a tenth of normal air pressure, across the floor of a car, holds it to the ceiling. Even when it's going slow.

[leave] But suction needs a surface. Leave the ramp, and nothing's holding you.

[thrust] So, the third way. Thrust.

[nozzles] Ten nozzles blast compressed air from about six hundred litres of tanks. Throw air one way, and the car goes the other. That's how it rolled over in mid-air.

[hover] So could it just hover? Here's the catch.

[seconds] Holding a car up takes something like thirty kilos of air, every second. Those tanks hold a few hundred at most. So you get seconds. Not minutes.

[heli] A helicopter hovers all day by pushing on the air around it. The Roadster carries its air in a bottle.

[hop] So it doesn't fly like a plane. It hops.

[real] What's real: a car that clings upside down and jumps on air thrusters, on a closed test site. What's still a promise: production in twenty twenty-eight, and a flying mode that isn't road legal.

[end] So… would you ride in it upside down?
