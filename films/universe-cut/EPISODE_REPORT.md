# Episode report — *What if the universe got cut in half?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 5:59 (359.15 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Universe faster
formula (the brief: that formula; the owner asked for the ~6-minute length because those films hold retention best).
The film is told by 79 short second-person captions, a corner readout (THE CUT IN → THE SHOCK REACHES YOU IN → SINCE
THE CUT, with one fact under it), a clock (TONIGHT, with fractions of a second in the replay), a context line, labels,
honest tags, a few diagrams and illustrations, and the sound. Page `universe-cut.html`, code `films/universe-cut/`,
every number in `PLAN.md` § 2 (computed in `physics.js` and `calc/`), captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The physical reading.** "The universe" can only mean the part we can see, and the observable universe is a ball
centred on us. A cut that halves a ball goes through its middle, so the cut goes through the Earth. The one free
choice, its tilt, is set so that it also goes through the real Moon tonight (28 October 2026, 10:00 PM GMT) and keeps
the Sun. Your garden in London is on the side that stays, 2,197 km from the cut; the edge runs across the Sahara.
Saturn, Uranus and Neptune are on the other side; Mercury, Venus, Mars and Jupiter stay. At 10:00:00 everything beyond
the cut vanishes, including the light in flight from it. What is left obeys ordinary physics:

- more than a third of your sky goes black at once (most stars within 0.04 s); the Moon is wiped in half between
  0.67 and 1.20 s (its light takes 1.2 s to reach you);
- gravity changes for you, the ground and the air at once and by the same amount, so you feel nothing;
- the rock does feel the uneven part of the change: small quakes start about a minute in, you get slightly heavier
  (+4.5 % by the shock, an estimate) and "down" tips 4° towards the cut; the air presses harder and your ears pop;
- the cut face is still at the temperature and pressure it had inside (5,400 °C, 3.6 million atmospheres) and bursts
  out at up to about 5 km/s, too slow to escape;
- the news travels through the rock as a shock wave (ray theory through PREM): Rome 10:02:07, Paris 10:03:24,
  **London 10:03:49**, New York 10:05:51, Tokyo 10:06:17;
- within an hour or two the half-Earth falls in on itself, melts and boils, a ball again about four-fifths as wide;
  the half-Moon is moving faster than half an Earth's escape speed (1,144 vs 1,052 m/s) and drifts away for good; the
  new Earth stays on almost the same orbit (the year about 3 days shorter); half the sky stays black for ever.

## Reviews

written the film. Scores as given:

| review | score | the main points |

| visuals | **6/10** | the aftermath planets read as a red sticker, then a ping-pong ball, and the Moon could be mistaken for the Earth; the edge read as a cloud bank over snow; the shocked ground looked like paving behind a neon tube; the street lamp passed for the Moon; the cut in the sky was too faint; the garden consequences (bird bath, car alarms, the shock) couldn't be seen; the face was one smooth gradient; too much small text; a dozen text collisions; long static holds |
| physics | **7/10** | sky, orbit, Moon-escape and face numbers reproduced; but you can't feel *lighter* before the shock (you get slightly heavier, "down" tipping towards the cut); quakes should start about a minute in; the arrival times were 5–7 s early (a min-over-a-box bug: London is 10:03:49, not 10:03:42); "no light will ever come" should be "no starlight"; only the *outer* core is liquid; the compass would not change for weeks (mantle filter); the telescope glow should be thin; "most" not "the rest" within 0.04 s; hedges on "within an hour" and "four-fifths" |

**What was changed after the reviews**

- *Physics.* Arrival times recomputed by ray theory (`calc/rays.py`): the shock reaches you at 10:03:49, Paris
  10:03:24, the count starts 10:03:37, and every caption, label, the readout and the closing question ("3 minutes and
  49 seconds") follow. "Lighter" became "heavier", with "down" tipping SSE towards the cut; the bird bath's water now
  rises on the SSE side. Small quakes from about 10:01:30 (the tea shivering, a low grumble growing under the garden,
  the ground trembling, the car alarms set off by the tremors rather than by tilt sensors). The compass beat was cut
  and replaced by the tea; a new "ears pop" beat (the air presses down with the felt weight). "No starlight",
  "outer core", "up to 13 km a second", "an hour or two", "about four-fifths", star counts rounded, "MOST WENT OUT
  WITHIN 0.04 SECONDS", the telescope glow tagged as drawn wider. A felt-weight and a tremor tag say these are
  estimates.
- *Story.* The cold open opens on the half-black sky and the half-Moon through the long lens ("Half the universe is
  gone. And you've barely felt a thing."); the weight readout stays on "HALF THE UNIVERSE: GONE" until 10:03; the rule
  is three captions with the ball-to-Earth step spelled out; "The Moon, replayed 8 times slower" with a label for its
  1.2 s light time; the replay keeps the main clock and countdown on real time and says "REPLAY 25×" in the clock's
  sub-line; "nowhere" folded away and a new beat, *the Sun made it* (the Sun, Mercury, Venus, Mars and Jupiter are on
  this side); the cat now notices; the shock gets two quick cuts (the feeder swinging, the cat bolting) before black.
- *Visuals.* The aftermath planets rebuilt (glowing cracks and lava under a dark crust lit by the Sun, then a steam
  shell with a terminator; a grey half-Moon with a glowing seam, smaller and behind); the edge rebuilt as a side-on view
  of the lip (the sea churned white, pouring over, the face glowing below); the shocked ground darkened with cracks,
  dust and its towns going out; the street lamp hooded; the stars and the Milky Way on the kept side brighter, the
  vanish and the line framed on the Milky Way; the face drawn in layers (crystalline inner core, swirling outer core,
  churning mantle, cold crust) on a cooler colour scale so they read; a new shot of the Sun beyond the half-Earth; the
  new Earth's path dotted in; the bird bath shot moved low and square to the tilt; amber hazard flashes on the terrace
  beyond the wall; inset text enlarged to 25–28 px; labels flip side instead of running off the frame; the collisions
  fixed (shock inset, front labels, Sun labels, space-station and earthquake insets).

A second preview after these fixes was checked frame by frame on contact sheets; no second round of independent
reviews was run.

## Sound

Synthesised and seeded (audiokit.js, sound-lib.js, sound-music.js, sound-amb.js, sound-sfx.js; mix.js): a score in E
whose upper strings stop dead at 10:00:00, a heartbeat under the countdown quickening into the shock, a cut-off bell
for things that are gone; the garden at night (far road, leaves, crickets, owls), the tremor grumble, the car alarms
and a dog, the spoon chattering, the ear-pop, the blow; a low hum in space. Baked into `soundtrack.js`.
Mix (rendered fresh from the final cut): **−15.8 LUFS integrated, −1.3 dBTP, LRA 8.6 LU**, building from about −18 dB
RMS at 10:03 to −12 at the shock. Encoded for the 1080p master: `final-soundtrack.m4a`, AAC 256k (−15.8 LUFS,
−1.3 dBTP). The same audio is baked into the page (`soundtrack.js`), so the ZIP plays it at once.

## What is still weak

- **The space shots are illustrations, not simulations.** The blow-out, the edge's air and sea, the collapse and the
  steam are drawn for the eye (tagged ILLUSTRATION, the time-lapses tagged, the Moon-leaving and Sun shots NOT TO
  SCALE). The face's colours are cooler than its true white glow so that the layers read.
- **Two numbers are estimates.** How much heavier you get before the shock (+4.5 %) and how strong the tremors are.
  The sign of the weight change and the arrival times are solid; the sizes are not. Both are tagged on screen.
- **The garden is stylised.** Simple houses and a low-poly cat; the bird bath's 2–3 cm rise and the feeder's 4° lean
  are true to scale, so they are subtle.
- **The real section is still four short diagrams over two garden backgrounds** (about 34 s).
- **No second independent review** was run after the fixes, so the scores above are for the first cut.
