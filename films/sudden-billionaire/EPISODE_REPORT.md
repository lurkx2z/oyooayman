# Episode report — *Would you actually be generous if you suddenly became a billionaire?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 7:52 (472.4 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Time on your feet
format (the owner's brief on 2026-10-26: that formula, about 8 minutes with no filler, real research on wealth,
generosity, lottery winners and hedonic adaptation, plus "sudden power and greed"). The film is told by 121 short
second-person captions, a corner readout and the sound. One number never stops moving: **your money, to the cent**,
from $412.37 to $1.30 billion, with GIVEN AWAY and a red STILL TO GIVE line that keeps climbing beside it. Page
`sudden-billionaire.html`, code `films/sudden-billionaire/`, every claim and its source in `PLAN.md` § 2, captions
`CAPTIONS.md`.

**The idea.** Your own story over ten years, with the research laid over it as it happens. Saturday night, $412 in the
bank, one lottery ticket; a billion dollars after tax, and a sticky note: *give half away*. Giving feels good (and the
studies say so, more modestly than first thought). Then everyone wants some, you stop trusting anyone, the money makes
$137,000 a day in your sleep, the house, the car, the neighbours going bankrupt keeping up, breakfast that stops tasting
special, wanting more, wanting to be ahead. Power: the boardroom, the cookie study, the rigged Monopoly game, a magazine
that calls you self-made, your politics drifting. The yacht and the bigger yacht, a wall and a guard, eyes off people
downtown, not stopping at the crossing. In year nine you find the note: half would be $627 million. Then the real
billionaires' pledge, the honest part (your story is made up; some studies were small or failed to repeat, the crossing
study among them), the opposite evidence (lost letters, payday, the surveys), MacKenzie Scott and Chuck Feeney. The same
crossing ten years on: this time you stop, and you see her. *So, be honest. Would you give half away?*

- Science: accuracy 6, caveats 6, balance 5, overall **6** ("about 8 if the fixes are made").

Fixed after it: every science wording and label (PLAN.md § 2), the crossing study's failed repeat on screen, a weak video
study replaced by MacKenzie Scott, the crossings restaged (she is big, lit and held), the night shot relit, the money built
from life-size bundles, the house warmed and labelled, HUD collisions and clipped panels, the STILL TO GIVE line.

**Second round** (one review of the fixed 7:46 cut), scores as given: hook 6, retention through the middle 5, clarity 7,
emotional payoff 5, image quality 6, composition 6, lighting/grade 6, polish 5, science honesty **8**, overall **6**.
It checked eight studies online and found them right except five wordings (fixed below).

Fixed after it (not re-scored): the sweets study (small, criticised, from the same paper as the failed crossing study)
replaced by a story beat, the magazine cover; "Of the 22 pledgers who have died, only 8 gave half away"; the letters
"with money inside" plus the payday finding; "one real woman's story began a lot like yours"; OVER 1,600 BY 2022; plainer
bankruptcy wording; the years chart now agrees with the readout; the WHERE clock follows the date; labels step aside under
diagrams; spaced and fitted panel text; a darker caption backing over the phone and on bright days; the garage shot no
longer starts above the car's roof; flares tamed; the city's windows no longer shimmer; the envelope wall readable before
it lights; the same woman at all three crossings; the note on the dashboard at the end.

**Not changed:** the second review's biggest suggestion, cutting about 70 s of studies for new story beats. The owner
asked for the research on sudden power and greed and set about 8 minutes, so the cookie and Monopoly studies stay, each
with an honest label.

**Still weak (honest):** the second round of fixes has not been re-reviewed. The middle (the lab studies, then the
pledge, letters and two real people) is the likeliest drop-off and still runs long without "you". People are the faceless
stylised rig. The Dunn lobby is very bright. The ending is quiet: nothing in the money changes when you stop. The sound
has been measured, not heard by a person.

## Sound

Synthesised, three stems (music, ambience, effects). A small piano "promise" figure (when you write the note, when you
find it, when you stop at the crossing); strings and a pulse that grow colder and busier as the money grows; the TV draw
and the balls; phone buzzes that become a swarm; a soft coin tick for the interest; cash rustle; the boardroom laugh; the
dice and the slapped board; a hollow fanfare for the magazine cover that sours; the yacht's chord and the bigger one;
the gate's clang; rain and wipers in the car; for MacKenzie Scott a quickening arpeggio and paper ticks as the envelopes
light. The middle is lifted by a dynamics map so phones hold it (the first measure had the open and the ending about
9 dB above it).

**Loudness (measured, ffmpeg ebur128):** mix −15.1 LUFS integrated, LRA 7.0 LU, true peak −1.5 dBTP (the first mix,
before the lift, was −17.5 LUFS with LRA 9.9). `final-soundtrack.m4a` (AAC 256k): −15.2 LUFS, −1.3 dBTP.
`small-soundtrack.m4a` (AAC 128k, limited first because low-bitrate AAC adds peaks): −15.5 LUFS, −3.3 dBTP. The page
plays the baked copy (`soundtrack.js`) at once (check-page: SOUND READY after 0 s, no errors, also from the unzipped ZIP).
A cloud render of this soundtrack takes about 11 minutes.
