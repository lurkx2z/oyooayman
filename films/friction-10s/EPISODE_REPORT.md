# Episode report — *What if friction disappeared for 10 seconds?*

**Format.** 16:9 long-form, 1920×1080, 30 fps, 5:02 (301.8 s). Voiceless, in the Slower light / Universe cut
formula, built to the owner's YouTube lessons and retention playbook of 28 October 2026. A fresh long-form, not a remake
of the old 9:16 Friction short. The film is told by 77 short second-person captions, a corner readout (FRICTION GOES IN
→ FRICTION BACK IN x.xx s → FRICTION BACK +x.xx s, with the speed under it: REAL SPEED, SLOWED DOWN 10×, REWIND,
REPLAY, FLASH-FORWARD), a clock (THIS MORNING 8:14:07 AM, hundredths in the replays), a context line, pinned labels,
honest tags, five small diagrams, and the sound. Page `friction-10s.html`, code `films/friction-10s/`, every number in
`PLAN.md` § 2 (computed in `physics.js`), captions `CAPTIONS.md`.

**The physical reading.** At 8:14:07 on a weekday morning every solid-on-solid contact above ground loses its sideways
grip (static and kinetic friction both zero) for exactly ten seconds. Things still push on each other, air still drags,
liquids are still liquids, gravity is unchanged. Friction is electromagnetic, and switching that force off completely
would also take solids apart, so the film removes only the snag and says so in the first minute. Underground every
fault would slip; the film says so and stays above ground. What follows is ordinary mechanics:

- anything held is squeezed out of your hand (holding is squeezing plus friction); anything moving keeps moving; no
  one can walk, brake, steer or balance a bike; knots, cotton yarn, wall plugs, scaffold clamps and tightened screws
  let go (a screw is a ramp wrapped round a pin, and a tightened one is being pulled along it);
- on a slope everything slides at a = g sin θ whatever its mass: your floor's 0.5° gives 0.0856 m/s², the street's
  8 % hill **0.782 m/s²**, so by 10 s everything on it is doing **28 km/h** and has slid **39 m** (cars, bins, people,
  sand almost exactly together; light boxy things 5–8 % behind, air drag);
- the bus was turning as it pulled out, so it keeps rotating (0.085 rad/s) and slides further sideways every second;
- at s = 10 friction comes back all at once: everything still moving stops in about a second (28 km/h → 0 in about
  1.3 s, like being thrown off a bike); the bus, sliding sideways, goes over (static stability factor ≈ 0.6 < μ 0.7),
  on its side by 11.25 s; cars (≈ 1.3 > μ) only rock on their springs; the fridge, sliding at 3 km/h, rocks up about
  5° on its front edge and drops back with a thud (it can't spin far enough to go over); the cat's paws grip
  mid-stride and she shoots onto the sofa.

## How it follows the owner's lessons and playbook

- **Everyday place, bizarre physics:** your kitchen at 8:14 on a weekday, the hill street outside; a cat, a coffee, a
  phone, a bus.
- **Action in the first second:** friction goes at 0:00.0 under the title; the title is fully up on frame 1.
- **The physics nuance in the first minute:** friction is electric (0:14), why only the snag goes (0:18), faults (0:28).
- **Escalation:** the kitchen → the house → the street → everything slides → the count down → s = 10 → after.
- **Open loops:** the flash-forward of the bus (0:10) pays off at 4:37 (from the same angle, and the bus is kept out of
  the s = 10 cuts so it isn't spoiled); "Getting it back is the part that hurts" pays off at 4:25; the phone heading for
  the door (0:37) pays off in the gutter (4:51).
- **A reset every 4–6 s:** caption, camera or clock change; the longest holds of the first cut are gone (see below).
- **Sudden silences:** the screech cut dead at 0:00; 0.42 s of total silence before s = 10.
- **No slow recap, no outro:** the film ends on the question, cut hard while it is still on screen.
- **Teaser Short:** 4:06–4:46 (40 s: your phone leaves the house, the warning, the count down, s = 10, the bus goes
  over; the second reviewer's pick too, needs a 9:16 reframe), or the cold open plus the flash-forward (0:00–0:13).

**What changed (the recut, 5:58 → 5:04):**

- every caption re-timed and most beats tightened (hold = 1 + 0.05 s a character, capped at 4.2 s); the cold open's
  hold on the oven handle cut to under 1.5 s, and "Friction just disappeared." brought in at 0:04 over a new low shot
  across the kitchen floor (you hooked on the oven handle, the cat, the broken mug, the phone); the nuance shortened
  and reworded (the same force holds your atoms; switch *all of it* off; faults in one line); "It's gone for 10
  seconds." instead of "It comes back in 10 seconds";
- the screw rewritten as the reviewer suggested ("A tightened screw is being pulled along. With no friction, it
  unwinds."), and the diagram now reads NO FRICTION: ANY PULL UNWINDS IT;
- tipping is now computed: each tall loose item's spin about its front edge at s = 10 decides whether it can go over.
  The fridge (and the bin) can't: they rock and drop back with a thud. Cars rock on their springs without lifting a
  wheel. The bus still goes over;
- s = 10 is now four hard cuts in 1.6 s at real speed, then the replays; the bus replay uses the low flash-forward angle
  so the opening loop visibly closes (moved back so the bus lands in frame, not on the camera); the count down has eight quick cuts round the house and the street and a
  tick-tock that speeds up;
- the 12 s replay of your phone split in two (your view, then a floor-level cut as it drops past your legs);
- cameras moved so the named thing is in frame: your hand on the island, the cat's legs, the TV followed down, the
  laces ("His shoelaces are untying themselves."), the walker, the sand (from above), your phone on the doorstep (its
  path down the steps fixed: it now slides along each tread and drops off its edge, with a clack on each landing),
  the real-speed pass at street level, the bus clear of the lamp post;
- the title sits higher (the cat and the coffee pass below it) and is up on frame 1; the film ends on a hard cut.

**Second review (the 5:04 recut, one fresh reviewer, contact sheets every 0.5 s):** **retention 6/10, clarity 7/10,
visuals 7/10** (up from 5, 6, 6). Its strongest moments: the bus going over (4:42), your feet shooting out with the cat in

- the closing readout vanished and the phone sat dark for 5.5 s, which signalled the end: the clock and the readout now

Not done (bigger changes, the reviewer's ideas): moving the physics nuance later, over the moving replay; cutting about

fixes were checked on stills, not re-reviewed, so the second scores are for the 5:04 recut.

## What is still weak

- The first 30 seconds set out the rule over nearly frozen frames (80× slower), and the middle (0:32–3:06) is a
  run of examples inside the same second and a half: both reviews' biggest retention worry.
- Much of the film is still slow motion by design (ten seconds told over five minutes). The cuts and the clock keep it
  moving, but some shots are still close to stills, and the retention score was the lowest of the three.
- The people are the engine's simple figures; the walker's fall at s = 10 reads oddly (upside down for a moment).
- Some street shots still have a lamp post or tree in a third of the frame (the wide cut at s = 10); some subjects
  are small (the dog, the scaffold), and the readout's small speed line is hard to read on a phone.
- The sand slumps outward more than it pours downhill in its slowed replay; it reads better at real speed.

## Sound

All synthesised and seeded (`sound-*.js`, `mix.js`; `js/audio/audioEngine.js` unchanged). Three stems (music, amb,
sfx), a dynamics map per stem, a true-peak limiter.

- the score (E minor): one low blow when friction goes; the chord glides down an octave as the clock slows (a tape
  stopping); a steady pulse under each replay section; the build from "Now everything starts to slide"; a tick-tock
  through the count down that speeds up; **0.4 s of total silence** (−80 dB) before s = 10, then the blow; the
  question's theme never resolves, and the film stops on it;
- the places: the kitchen and the street at real speed, and the same places stretched (darker, an octave down) when
  slowed down; the bus's diesel until it goes over; car alarms all along the street after;
- the events at their story seconds, the same sound in every replay, slowed down with the picture: the brakes' screech
  cut dead at s = 0; nothing scrapes or skids while friction is gone (things slide in silence); your phone's clack on
  each step; at s = 10 every tyre at once, the bodies, the bins, the fridge's thud, the bus going over.

Measured (EBU R128): **−15.5 LUFS integrated, loudness range 7.8 LU, true peak −1.5 dBTP** (the mastered WAV);
the final AAC 256k soundtrack measures −15.5 LUFS and −1.2 dBTP. Target −16 to −14 LUFS, ≤ −1 dBTP: met.
