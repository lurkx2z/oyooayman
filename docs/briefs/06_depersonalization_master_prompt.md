# What weed-induced depersonalization can feel like — master build prompt

> (chat-only lines like "send the video when done" kept as written). Where a newer brief or `docs/` disagrees, the newer one wins.

````text
MASTER BUILD PROMPT
PROJECT: WHAT WEED-INDUCED DEPERSONALIZATION CAN FEEL LIKE
FORMAT: 75–90 SECOND CINEMATIC POV EXPERIENCE
ENGINE: HTML + JavaScript + Three.js / WebGL

==================================================
ROLE
==================================================

You are my:

- lead Three.js developer
- technical director
- cinematic director
- environment artist
- sound designer
- first-person POV designer
- emotional experience designer
- retention editor
- production planner

Your job is to BUILD the actual experience.

Do not merely explain how it could be done.

I am a beginner.

Keep manual work minimal.

Write and maintain the code yourself.

==================================================
PROJECT CONCEPT
==================================================

TITLE:

WHAT WEED-INDUCED DEPERSONALIZATION CAN FEEL LIKE

This is part of a broader “What It Feels Like” series.

The purpose is to simulate the subjective experience in a respectful, understandable, cinematic way.

This is NOT:

- anti-weed propaganda
- a horror short about “going crazy”
- a joke
- a sensationalized “bad trip” monster video
- a claim that this happens to everyone who uses cannabis

This IS:

a stylized simulation of what depersonalization and derealization can feel like for some people after weed triggers it.

The experience should feel:

unsettling
disconnected
strange
emotionally distant
internally distressing
but visually grounded in ordinary reality

The most important idea:

THE WORLD DOES NOT TURN INTO A MONSTER MOVIE.

Everything stays basically normal.

The terror comes from:

feeling disconnected from yourself,
and feeling like reality has become emotionally unfamiliar or unreal.

==================================================
MEDICAL / ETHICAL FRAMING
==================================================

Treat this responsibly.

Important truths:

- cannabis can trigger depersonalization/derealization in some people
- it does not happen to everyone
- this does not mean the person is “crazy”
- the experience can be frightening
- symptoms can be worsened by anxiety and panic
- people can experience it differently

Do NOT portray the person as violent, psychotic, evil, or out of control.

Do NOT use demon imagery, monsters, shadow creatures, or exaggerated hallucinations.

Do NOT equate depersonalization with schizophrenia or psychosis.

This video is about:
feeling detached from yourself and/or reality.

At the end, include a gentle non-alarmist note such as:

“If these feelings continue after the high wears off, talk to a healthcare professional.”

This should be brief and unobtrusive.

==================================================
FORMAT
==================================================

Target duration:
75–90 seconds.

Preferred target:
around 85 seconds.

Aspect ratio:
9:16 vertical.

Native resolution:
1080 × 1920.

Frame rate:
30 fps.

Designed for:
TikTok
YouTube Shorts
Instagram Reels

==================================================
VISUAL STYLE
==================================================

Use the same established channel rendering philosophy:

POLISHED CINEMATIC LOW-POLY FIRST-PERSON SIMULATION

Do NOT make this look like:
- a browser coding demo
- a bright Roblox game
- a horror game
- a psychedelic monster trip video
- cheap glitch effects spam

Instead aim for:

- atmospheric
- grounded
- modern
- carefully art-directed
- clean but cinematic
- emotionally unsettling through subtle distortion
- stylized but believable

Use the existing POV style foundation from the previous projects:

- elegant serif captions
- minimal HUD
- cinematic vignette
- strong foreground/midground/background separation
- authored low-poly assets
- restrained post-processing
- clear first-person immersion

==================================================
CORE EXPERIENCE DESIGN
==================================================

The key emotional progression:

NORMAL
↓
SLIGHTLY OFF
↓
SELF FEELS UNFAMILIAR
↓
WORLD FEELS UNREAL
↓
ANXIETY LOCKS ONTO IT
↓
REALITY-CHECKING LOOP
↓
QUIET COMEDOWN / GENTLE REASSURANCE

The viewer should feel:

“Nothing is obviously wrong… so why does everything feel wrong?”

That is the core of the video.

==================================================
IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE: DEPERSONALIZATION VS DEREALIZATION
==================================================

This video may include both, but the central focus is:

DEPERSONALIZATION:
feeling detached from yourself, your body, your thoughts, your emotions, or your reflection.

DEREALIZATION:
feeling like the world around you is dreamlike, distant, flat, artificial, or emotionally unreal.

The experience should blur between them naturally, because many people describe both together.

==================================================
TITLE / OPENING QUESTION
==================================================

VERY IMPORTANT:

The main title/question must be:

MORE PROMINENT
and
ON SCREEN LONGER

than in the oxygen video.

Reason:
this topic is less visually self-explanatory.

The viewer needs immediate clarity about what the video is about.

Use the title:

WHAT WEED-INDUCED
DEPERSONALIZATION
CAN FEEL LIKE

Display it clearly for long enough that a casual viewer can actually read and understand it.

Guidelines:

- title should appear within the first second
- remain visible longer than the oxygen title
- not too huge, but clearly more prominent
- still elegant and cinematic
- serif or refined typography
- high readability on phone screens
- should not vanish too quickly

The title is not merely branding.
It is a comprehension tool.

==================================================
POV / CAMERA
==================================================

First-person POV.

Camera height:
adult / young adult.

Use:

- subtle breathing
- restrained head sway
- natural turning
- seated posture at start
- gradual instability later
- slight hesitation in movement
- moments of staring
- increased self-focused attention

Do NOT overdo shake.

The camera should feel human, not like a handheld horror cam.

The experience should feel:
tense and intimate,
not chaotic.

==================================================
FIRST-PERSON BODY
==================================================

If a good first-person arm/hand asset is available, use it.

This is valuable for this specific concept.

Hands are important because the viewer needs to look at their own body and feel disconnected from it.

Important hand/body moments:

- resting hands at the beginning
- looking down at hands when things start to feel off
- touching face/chest/arm
- hand in mirror view
- possible trembling later
- maybe gripping sink/counter/chair while grounding

If no good asset exists:
use only when necessary, but avoid ugly placeholder hands.

Bad hands will break immersion.

==================================================
SETTING
==================================================

Use an ordinary, believable modern setting.

Suggested setting progression:

1. living room / bedroom / friend's apartment
2. bathroom / mirror
3. hallway or kitchen
4. maybe brief outside / balcony / street / convenience store feel
5. return to quieter space

The environment should feel normal and ordinary.

That contrast is essential.

Do NOT make the world look inherently scary.

Normal spaces should become subjectively strange.

==================================================
CHARACTERS
==================================================

There may be one or two other people in the scene, for example friends.

They should act normal.

That contrast increases the depersonalization feeling.

Examples:

- friend talking casually
- someone asking “you good?”
- someone laughing or speaking normally
- another person moving naturally while POV feels weird

Do NOT turn the friends into villains or monsters.

They should remain ordinary.

The discomfort comes from the POV’s altered perception.

==================================================
SOUND DESIGN
==================================================

This project will live or die by sound.

Use sound very carefully.

Beginning:
- soft room tone
- distant music
- ordinary conversation
- subtle ambient hum

As symptoms begin:
- friend’s voice feels slightly farther away
- mild muffling
- slight internal ringing/hiss
- breathing becomes more noticeable
- heartbeat can gradually enter later

Important:
do NOT use cheap horror stingers.

The sound should become:
strangely detached,
slightly unreal,
overly internal.

Possible techniques:
- slight reverb shift
- mild muffling
- reduced high-frequency clarity
- attention focus on breathing/heartbeat
- sound lag feeling
- room ambience thinning out

As anxiety increases:
- heartbeat slightly louder
- breathing tighter
- internal thoughts may feel more prominent than the environment

End:
- breathing slows
- ambience becomes clearer again
- not necessarily “fully cured,” but calmer

==================================================
VISUAL EFFECT LANGUAGE
==================================================

All effects should be SUBTLE and PSYCHOLOGICALLY PLAUSIBLE.

Good effects:
- slight desaturation
- mild depth weirdness
- slight vignette tightening
- soft edge blur
- subtle temporal lag feel
- gentle focus breathing
- slightly strange motion smoothing
- reflection discomfort
- mild lighting detachment
- distant-feeling people
- tunnel-like attention

Do NOT use:
- screaming faces
- aggressive glitch spam
- demons
- melting walls
- heavy psychedelic fractals
- impossible geometry
- blood / gore
- horror monsters
- obvious “crazy vision” clichés

Everything should remain mostly ordinary.

The more normal the world looks,
the stronger the experience becomes.

==================================================
HUD / INFORMATION DESIGN
==================================================

Use minimal HUD.

This is not a scientific simulation like oxygen,
but a small amount of internal-state UI can help.

Possible tasteful HUD elements:

SENSE OF SELF
100% → lower

REALITY FEEL
NORMAL → DISTANT

ANXIETY
LOW → RISING

or

SELF
CONNECTED → DISCONNECTED

Use sparingly.

Do NOT turn this into a game stats screen.

The main explanatory tools should be:
- title
- serif captions
- camera
- sound
- subtle effects

==================================================
SERIF CAPTION STYLE
==================================================

Use elegant, restrained captions.

One thought at a time.

Examples:

“At first, nothing seems wrong.”

“Then something feels… off.”

“You know those are your hands.”

“They just don’t feel like yours.”

“The world still looks normal.”

“It just stops feeling normal.”

“The more you check…”

“…the stranger it can feel.”

These should be short, emotionally precise, and spaced well.

Avoid overexplaining.

==================================================
FULL STORY / TIMELINE
==================================================

--------------------------------------------------
0:00–0:08 — CLEAR HOOK / TITLE
--------------------------------------------------

Start in a normal room with friends after smoking or being high.

Do not glamorize or over-focus on the smoking act itself.

The state can be implied rather than heavily shown.

Immediately establish the title:

WHAT WEED-INDUCED
DEPERSONALIZATION
CAN FEEL LIKE

Leave it on screen longer than the oxygen title.

Normal room ambience.
Nothing alarming yet.

A friend says something casual.

POV seems okay.

Caption:
“At first, everything feels normal.”

--------------------------------------------------
0:08–0:18 — SOMETHING SHIFTS
--------------------------------------------------

A small strange feeling begins.

POV hears a friend’s voice, but it feels slightly distant or odd.

POV looks down at hands.

The hands are visibly normal.

But something feels wrong.

Subtle HUD:
SENSE OF SELF
100% → 78%

Caption:
“Then something starts to feel… off.”

Very important:
the effect should be uncomfortable, not dramatic.

--------------------------------------------------
0:18–0:30 — YOUR OWN BODY FEELS UNFAMILIAR
--------------------------------------------------

POV stands or walks to mirror / bathroom / reflective surface.

Looks into mirror.

Reflection matches perfectly.

But emotionally, it does not feel like “me.”

POV raises hand.
Reflection does too.

Caption:
“You know it’s you.”

Beat.

“It just doesn’t feel like you.”

This is the core depersonalization moment.

Use:
mirror
hands
face
breathing

But do NOT turn the reflection into a horror effect.

No independent movement.
No scary face.

Just emotional estrangement.

--------------------------------------------------
0:30–0:42 — THE WORLD FEELS UNREAL
--------------------------------------------------

POV exits room / enters hallway or another ordinary space.

The world is still objectively normal.

But it feels:
flat
dreamlike
distant
artificial
slightly “off”

Caption:
“The world can start feeling dreamlike…”

“…or weirdly far away.”

Possible subtle effects:
- sound more muffled
- slight edge blur
- reduced emotional warmth in scene
- mild time weirdness
- altered sense of distance

Again:
no monsters.
no surreal hallucination overload.

--------------------------------------------------
0:42–0:54 — TIME AND ATTENTION GET WEIRD
--------------------------------------------------

POV checks phone / clock.

Looks away.

Looks back.

Very little time has passed,
but it feels longer.

Could use:
TIME SINCE CHECK
00:12

Caption:
“Even a few seconds can feel strangely long.”

A friend may say:
“You good?”

The voice sounds normal but emotionally far away.

This increases discomfort.

--------------------------------------------------
0:54–1:06 — PANIC MAKES IT WORSE
--------------------------------------------------

This is crucial.

The experience itself becomes scary,
and anxiety starts feeding it.

POV starts reality-checking:

- looking at hands again
- checking mirror again
- checking breathing
- checking phone/clock
- maybe touching wall / face / chest

HUD:
ANXIETY
RISING

Caption:
“The fear can make it stronger.”

This should communicate the feedback loop.

--------------------------------------------------
1:06–1:18 — THE LOOP
--------------------------------------------------

Core message:

the more you keep checking whether you feel normal,
the more abnormal everything starts to feel.

Make this the emotional climax.

Quick loop montage:
hands
mirror
room
friend
clock
breathing
hands again

Caption:
“The more you check…”

beat

“…the stranger everything can feel.”

This may be the strongest line in the whole video.

Make it land.

--------------------------------------------------
1:18–1:28 — QUIET COMEDOWN / GROUNDING
--------------------------------------------------

POV sits somewhere quieter.

Maybe a friend sits nearby calmly.

No instant miracle.

Just a slight reduction in intensity.

Breathing starts slowing.

Sound becomes a bit clearer.

World is still a little strange,
but less overwhelming.

Caption:
“It can feel terrifying…”

“…even when nothing around you has changed.”

--------------------------------------------------
1:28–1:35 — ENDING / RESPONSIBLE NOTE
--------------------------------------------------

End with a gentle, grounded close.

Small final text:

“For some people, it fades as the high wears off.”

Then a smaller final note:

“If it keeps happening or doesn’t go away,
talk to a healthcare professional.”

Do not make this feel like a warning ad.
Keep it simple and respectful.

Fade out.

==================================================
IMPORTANT PACING NOTES
==================================================

The entire video should feel like a steady tightening of discomfort.

Not:
normal → insane chaos.

Instead:
normal
→ slightly off
→ disconnected
→ unreal
→ anxious
→ looping
→ calmer

Every section should progress the feeling.

Do not stay too long in one beat.

==================================================
IMPORTANT CONTENT RULES
==================================================

Do NOT imply everyone who smokes weed will experience this.

Do NOT make it a moral panic video.

Do NOT mock people who experience this.

Do NOT present it as entertainment alone; treat it with emotional seriousness.

Do NOT over-pathologize normal cannabis effects either.

We are showing one possible difficult experience.

POST-PROCESSING
==================================================

Use existing rendering systems as foundation.

Effects should be:
- precise
- subtle
- layered

Possible stack:
- mild vignette
- soft focus breathing
- subtle color desaturation shift
- slight contrast shift
- light grain
- gentle audio-visual detachment effects

No cheap horror effects.

REUSE EXISTING ENGINE
==================================================

Reuse where possible:

- sound manager

This is a new story,
not a new rendering philosophy.

==================================================

Build in phases.

PHASE 1:

- title
- first signs
- looking at hands

18–42 seconds

PHASE 3:

PHASE 4:
66–85 seconds

==================================================
QUALITY REVIEW
==================================================

Use independent reviewers if available.

Reviewer roles:
1. normal viewer
2. retention reviewer
3. mental-health sensitivity reviewer
4. cinematography/3D reviewer

Ask them:

- Is the topic immediately understandable?
- Does the title stay visible long enough?
- Does the video feel respectful?
- Does it avoid horror clichés?
- Is the feeling understandable even to someone who has never experienced it?
- What moment is strongest?
- What moment is confusing?
- At what second would viewers likely swipe?
- What looks cheapest or least immersive?

Score:
HOOK
CLARITY
RESPECTFULNESS
VISUAL QUALITY
POV IMMERSION
EMOTIONAL ACCURACY
RETENTION POTENTIAL
OVERALL IMPACT

Do not inflate scores.

==================================================
SUCCESS CRITERIA
==================================================

A viewer who has experienced this should think:

“Yeah… that’s actually kind of what it felt like.”

A viewer who has not experienced this should think:

“Oh. So that’s why it can be so scary.”

The video should feel:
clear,
responsible,
emotionally accurate,
cinematic,
and memorable.
````
