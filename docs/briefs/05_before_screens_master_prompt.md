````text
MASTER BUILD PROMPT
PROJECT: HOW DID KIDS HAVE FUN BEFORE SCREENS?
FORMAT: 90-SECOND CINEMATIC POV HISTORY SIMULATION
ENGINE: HTML + JavaScript + Three.js / WebGL

==================================================
ROLE
==================================================

You are my:

- lead Three.js developer
- technical director
- cinematographer
- low-poly environment artist
- historical visualization designer
- VFX designer
- sound planner
- short-form retention editor
- production manager

Your job is to BUILD the actual 90-second experience.

Do not merely explain how it could be created.

I am a beginner, so when I need to do something manually:
- explain exactly what to do
- tell me what file to open
- tell me what to click/run
- keep manual work minimal

Write and maintain the code yourself.

==================================================
PROJECT CONCEPT
==================================================

TITLE:

HOW DID KIDS HAVE FUN BEFORE SCREENS?

Alternative short title for on-screen use:

BEFORE SCREENS...

Core idea:

A modern kid's phone dies.

Instead of giving a documentary lecture, we physically transport the viewer into a stylized pre-screen childhood world.

The viewer experiences:

- street games
- marbles
- spinning tops
- homemade toys
- kite flying
- imagination-based play
- group games
- storytelling after dark

The video should feel like:

POV SIMULATION
+
HISTORY
+
NOSTALGIA
+
CURIOSITY
+
CINEMATIC STORYTELLING

The goal is NOT:

"Kids in the past had better lives."

The goal is:

"With fewer ready-made forms of entertainment, kids often turned ordinary places, objects and other people into play."

Do not become preachy or anti-technology.

==================================================
HISTORICAL FRAMING
==================================================

Use a broad visual setting inspired primarily by:

late 19th century to early 20th century pre-screen childhood.

We are NOT claiming that every child, country or social class lived identically.

Childhood differed enormously depending on:

- country
- class
- urban vs rural environment
- period
- family income
- work responsibilities

The video is a stylized composite illustrating common forms of play.

Do NOT state:

"All children did this."

Prefer wording such as:

"Many children..."
"Games like these..."
"For kids who had free time..."
"On streets and in yards..."

Historical accuracy matters, but the narration should remain short and entertaining.

==================================================
FORMAT
==================================================

Duration:
approximately 85–95 seconds.

Target:
90 seconds.

Aspect:
9:16 vertical.

Design natively for:
1080 × 1920.

Frame rate:
30 fps.

Primary platforms:

TikTok
YouTube Shorts
Instagram Reels

Everything important must remain readable on a phone.

==================================================
PRIMARY VISUAL TARGET
==================================================

Use the graphical language we established from the provided references.

STYLE MIX:

approximately 70% polished POV What-If reference
approximately 30% Omaha reference atmosphere.

Overall description:

POLISHED CINEMATIC LOW-POLY FIRST-PERSON SIMULATION.

NOT:
- photorealistic
- Roblox-looking
- Pixar
- colorful mobile-game graphics
- generic Three.js coding-demo graphics
- hyper-dirty PS2 recreation

==================================================
VISUAL CHARACTER
==================================================

Target:

- authored low/mid-poly geometry
- strong silhouettes
- atmospheric depth
- moderate fog/haze
- stylized but convincing materials
- soft cinematic lighting
- controlled color palette
- clean but not sterile
- strong foreground / midground / background separation
- clear first-person scale
- minimal elegant HUD
- restrained serif narration

IMPORTANT:

The historical world should look ALIVE and inviting.

Do NOT automatically apply:
sepia
black-and-white
brown vintage filters.

The past should contain:

green grass
blue/grey sky
warm sunlight
painted wooden buildings
muted clothing
warm fires/candles
colorful kites
glass marbles

Make it feel like an actual world rather than an old photograph.

==================================================
LIGHTING DIRECTION
==================================================

Unlike the oxygen project, this video can begin warmer.

DAYTIME:

- soft morning / afternoon sun
- atmospheric haze
- pleasant warmth
- readable shadows
- vibrant but controlled color

EVENING:

- orange sunset
- longer shadows
- cooler surrounding environment

NIGHT INTERIOR:

- candle/fireplace warmth
- dark exterior
- strong warm/cool contrast

The day-to-night progression should make the video visually evolve.

==================================================
FIRST-PERSON POV
==================================================

Viewer is the child.

Camera height should be lower than adult height.

Target:

approximately 1.25–1.4 meters.

The camera should feel like a child around roughly 9–12 years old.

Use:

- subtle breathing
- light head sway
- natural walking
- occasional running
- natural reaction turns
- looking down at hands
- interacting with objects
- reaching
- throwing
- kneeling
- standing up

Do NOT exaggerate FPS camera bob.

Movement should feel natural and cinematic.

==================================================
FIRST-PERSON BODY
==================================================

A good first-person body is highly valuable.

If we have an authored low-poly child arm/hand asset:

use it.

Hands should appear for:

- holding phone
- picking up marbles
- spinning top
- holding stick
- building toy
- flying kite
- putting phone down at ending

Do NOT use blocky procedural mitten hands.

If no suitable hand asset exists:

use minimal body visibility until a proper asset is available.

Bad hands are worse than no hands.

==================================================
RETENTION PHILOSOPHY
==================================================

Every 2–4 seconds, something meaningful should change.

The viewer should constantly receive:

NEW OBJECT
NEW GAME
NEW LOCATION
NEW VISUAL TRANSFORMATION
NEW QUESTION
NEW SOCIAL INTERACTION
NEW TIME-OF-DAY CHANGE

Avoid long walking sequences.

Do not spend 8 seconds watching one game.

This is not a documentary.

It is a rapid cinematic simulation.

==================================================
FULL 90-SECOND TIMELINE
==================================================

--------------------------------------------------
0:00–0:04 — HOOK
--------------------------------------------------

Modern bedroom / couch.

POV looking at phone.

Phone battery:

1%

Short modern ambience.

Phone shuts off.

Screen goes black.

POV taps it.

Nothing.

On-screen text:

NO PHONE.
NO TV.
NO INTERNET.

Then:

SO WHAT DID KIDS DO?

Keep text minimal and elegant.

Do not cover the whole screen.

Sound:

phone shutdown
room ambience
brief silence

Then a transition begins.

--------------------------------------------------
0:04–0:10 — ENTER THE PAST
--------------------------------------------------

POV looks up.

Modern room transforms / match-cuts into an older environment.

Potential transition:

camera passes doorway
→ modern hallway
→ same movement reveals historical street

Or:

lights flicker
→ sound drops
→ door opens
→ past world outside

Reveal:

historic neighborhood / town street.

Children visible playing outside.

No screens.

No modern cars.

No neon signage.

No modern plastic clutter.

Narration:

"So what did kids actually do all day?"

The environment should immediately answer:

They went outside.

--------------------------------------------------
0:10–0:20 — THE STREET WAS THE PLAYGROUND
--------------------------------------------------

POV steps outside.

Children run past.

One group plays tag.

Another runs with hoops.

Another plays with a ball.

Camera joins briefly.

Narration:

"For many kids, the playground wasn't a screen."

Beat.

"It was the street."

Show motion.

Do not stand still.

Potential visual:

child runs directly past POV
→ camera turns
→ group races down lane

Use dust, footsteps, laughter, cloth motion.

--------------------------------------------------
0:20–0:31 — MARBLES / TOP / JACKS
--------------------------------------------------

Match cut downward.

POV kneels.

Ground-level cinematic shot.

A small circle scratched/drawn into dirt or pavement.

Marbles.

POV hand flicks marble.

Satisfying:

CLICK.

Marble hits another.

Quick transition:

spinning top.

POV launches it.

Top spins rapidly.

Optional third quick beat:

jacks / small hand game.

Narration:

"Sometimes the entire game fit in your pocket."

Then:

"A few marbles could become a whole afternoon."

Do NOT literally keep the same toy for an afternoon.

This is storytelling.

Important:

physics should be satisfying.

Marbles need:

- momentum
- collision
- slight rolling variation
- glass/specular appearance

Top needs:

- believable spin
- gradual wobble
- slowing

--------------------------------------------------
0:31–0:43 — IF YOU DIDN'T HAVE A TOY, MAKE ONE
--------------------------------------------------

POV approaches kids building something.

Objects:

wood
string
paper
cloth
small wheels

Show a very quick assembly montage.

Do NOT simulate ten minutes of crafting.

Use cinematic jump cuts.

Reveal:

KITE.

Narration:

"And if you didn't own the toy you wanted..."

Beat.

"...you could make one."

Kids run.

POV holds string.

Kite rises.

Camera tilts upward.

Beautiful sky shot.

This should be a major visual relief / scale moment.

Wind sound.

Fabric flutter.

String tension.

--------------------------------------------------
0:43–0:56 — IMAGINATION
--------------------------------------------------

POV lowers kite / transition.

POV picks up ordinary wooden stick.

Narration:

"And sometimes..."

"...the toy wasn't really the point."

POV looks at stick.

For a moment:

just a stick.

Then imagination transformation begins.

ENVIRONMENT MORPHS.

Street becomes:

castle courtyard / medieval battleground.

Stick becomes visually represented as:

sword.

Other children become:

knights / adventurers.

IMPORTANT:

This should clearly represent imagination.

It must NOT imply magic literally happened.

Use a visual cue:

color wash
sound transition
brief fantasy overlay

POV sword clash.

1–2 seconds of exciting fantasy play.

Then:

SMASH CUT.

Back to reality.

The viewer is still holding the wooden stick.

Narration:

"A stick could become anything."

This should be one of the most memorable parts of the video.

--------------------------------------------------
0:56–1:08 — GAMES WERE SOCIAL
--------------------------------------------------

POV sees larger group.

Rapid interconnected sequence:

- jump rope
- hide-and-seek
- hopscotch
- ball game
- chasing

Do not give each one a separate scene.

Use quick 1–2 second beats.

Minimal HUD:

PLAYERS
12

or:

KIDS PLAYING
12

Only if it looks natural.

Narration:

"And most games had something else built in..."

Pause.

"...other kids."

Do NOT frame this as "modern children have no friends."

It is simply highlighting the social nature of group play.

--------------------------------------------------
1:08–1:20 — EVENING
--------------------------------------------------

Time transition.

Sun begins falling rapidly.

Warm golden light.

Children head home.

Street becomes quieter.

POV enters warm interior.

Fireplace / lamp / candle.

Family / children gathered.

Someone tells story.

Narration:

"When the sun went down..."

"...the entertainment changed."

Show:

storytelling
book
simple cards / board game
drawing

Do not cram everything into one frame.

Use a few quick beats.

--------------------------------------------------
1:20–1:27 — STORIES BECOME WORLDS
--------------------------------------------------

This is second imagination beat.

Story being told.

Shadows on wall begin taking forms:

dragon
ship
castle
forest

Do NOT make this horror.

Beautiful stylized imagination.

Narration:

"A story could build an entire world..."

Camera pushes slowly toward shadows.

"...without a screen."

This is the emotional high point.

--------------------------------------------------
1:27–1:33 — RETURN
--------------------------------------------------

Hard sound transition.

PHONE VIBRATION.

Back to modern bedroom.

Phone has restarted / is charging.

Notifications:

47 notifications

Do not copy real social media logos unless necessary.

Use generic notifications.

POV picks up phone.

Looks at screen.

Then hears:

children laughing outside.

Camera slowly turns toward window.

--------------------------------------------------
1:33–1:30? CORRECT FINAL TIMING
--------------------------------------------------

IMPORTANT:
Total video must remain about 90 seconds.

Compress the final sections as needed.

Recommended actual end:

1:24–1:30.

FINAL SHOT:

POV looks:

phone
→ window
→ phone

Hand places phone FACE-DOWN.

POV opens door.

Bright outside light.

CUT TO BLACK.

Final small serif line:

"Maybe boredom had a purpose."

OR preferably slightly less preachy:

"Sometimes boredom became the game."

Choose whichever feels less moralizing.

END.

==================================================
TIMING CORRECTION
==================================================

The above beats are conceptual.

Build the final timeline so the total duration is approximately:

88–92 seconds.

Do not exceed 95 seconds.

Target:

90 seconds.

The editor should compress or slightly overlap sections rather than cutting important beats.

==================================================
NARRATION STYLE
==================================================

Narration should be extremely economical.

No textbook language.

No long paragraphs.

Target short lines.

Example:

"So what did kids actually do?"

"For many of them, the street was the playground."

"Sometimes the entire game fit in your pocket."

"If you didn't have a toy, you made one."

"A stick could become anything."

"And when the sun went down..."

"...stories took over."

Do not narrate everything visible.

Show first.

Explain second.

==================================================
HISTORICAL ACCURACY
==================================================

Before implementing major historical props, sanity-check them.

Look for obvious anachronisms.

Avoid:

- modern cars
- modern smartphones
- plastic modern toys
- LED lighting
- modern street signs
- contemporary clothing
- current storefront branding

Depending on chosen setting, acceptable assets may include:

- bicycles
- wooden hoops
- marbles
- spinning tops
- handmade kites
- simple balls
- rope
- wooden carts
- early street lamps
- brick / timber buildings

The final historical setting does NOT need to represent one exact city.

But it must feel internally consistent.

==================================================
WORLD DESIGN
==================================================

Build one reusable historical neighborhood.

It should contain:

- street
- houses
- yards
- fences
- trees
- alleys
- small open field
- interiors
- windows
- doors
- street lamps
- benches
- barrels/crates
- carts/bicycles where appropriate

Use strong depth.

Foreground:
props / characters.

Midground:
games.

Background:
houses / trees.

Far:
hazy town silhouettes.

==================================================
CHARACTERS
==================================================

Do not use capsule mannequins.

Use authored-looking low-poly child models.

Need approximately:

6–10 child appearance variants.

Variation through:

- height
- clothing
- hats
- hair
- body shape
- colors
- accessories

You can reuse base meshes intelligently.

Animations:

- idle
- walk
- run
- crouch
- throw
- jump
- kneel
- play
- pull rope
- point
- laugh reaction
- hide

Animation quality matters more than face detail.

Faces can remain extremely simple.

==================================================
CHARACTER BEHAVIOR
==================================================

Characters should not all animate in sync.

Randomize:

- start times
- speeds
- idle duration
- turns
- reactions

Children should feel like individuals, not clones.

==================================================
TREES / VEGETATION
==================================================

Use authored-looking low-poly vegetation.

Avoid:

- spheres on sticks
- piles of random triangle shards

Use:

- irregular canopy masses
- 3–5 tree variants
- bushes
- grass patches
- flowers where appropriate

Warm historical world.

==================================================
MATERIAL STYLE
==================================================

Use coherent stylized materials.

Examples:

WOOD:
rough, warm, simple grain.

BRICK:
muted red/brown.

STONE:
grey/beige.

CLOTHING:
mostly matte.

METAL:
selective low-level highlights.

GLASS MARBLES:
stronger specular/refraction if affordable.

KITE:
paper/cloth matte surface.

FIRE:
warm emissive.

Do not mix photoreal textures with extremely simple cartoon meshes.

Everything should belong to one visual world.

==================================================
FOG / ATMOSPHERIC DEPTH

Foreground:
clear.

light haze.

soft silhouettes.

subtle blue/black exterior haze.

==================================================
DETERMINISTIC TIMELINE
==================================================

Playback must be deterministic.

Use a seeded random generator.

The same run should produce:

same character placement
same game timing
same kite movement
same camera path
same particles

This is important for recording and debugging.

==================================================
PHYSICS
==================================================

Real physics only where useful.

Use simplified deterministic animation when easier.

Physics worth implementing:

marbles
spinning top
ball
small thrown objects
kite motion

Do NOT waste time on:

full cloth simulation
complex crowd physics
perfect sword collision
realistic fluid simulation

Fake what viewers cannot distinguish.

==================================================
TRANSITIONS
==================================================

Use cinematic transitions to avoid loading huge scenes simultaneously.

Examples:

doorway
whip pan
camera looks down
object passes close
bright sky
shadow
match cut
eyes blink
sound bridge

Important transition:

MODERN ROOM → HISTORICAL STREET

should feel magical but controlled.

The final return should mirror it.

==================================================
PERFORMANCE
==================================================

Target:

30 FPS minimum on the current desktop.

Use:

InstancedMesh
shared materials
LOD
reused character rigs
low-poly assets
fog hiding distant low detail

Do not load extremely large GLB files.

Prefer visual quality per polygon.

==================================================

==================================================

Use proper GLB/GLTF assets for hero objects.

Priority:

1. child characters
2. first-person arms
3. historical buildings
4. kite
5. bicycles / carts
6. toys
7. trees
8. interior furniture

Do NOT procedurally build hero assets from crude boxes if proper low-poly assets are available.

Procedural geometry is fine for:

distant buildings
ground
simple fences
background props
invisible collision geometry

==================================================
CONTENT RULE
==================================================

This is NOT:

"old times good, phones bad."

Avoid simplistic moralizing.

We should acknowledge implicitly:

historical childhood could also involve:
- chores
- work
- fewer possessions
- less free time for some children

HOW CHILDREN PLAYED WITHOUT SCREENS.

==================================================
ENDING TONE
==================================================

Ending should be reflective.

NOT:

"phones destroyed childhood."

Boredom often forced kids to create their own entertainment.

No lecture.

Let the action make the point.

==================================================
SUCCESS CRITERIA
==================================================

Viewer should think:

0–3 sec:
"Oh, that's actually interesting."

10 sec:
"So that's what they did?"

30 sec:

50 sec:

"Maybe I'd actually go outside."

The video should feel like a miniature cinematic experience, not an educational slideshow.
````
