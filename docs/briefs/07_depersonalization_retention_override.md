````text
RETENTION OVERRIDE — THIS PROJECT MUST BE EDITED LIKE A VIRAL SHORT, NOT A SHORT FILM

The previous creative direction remains valid, but RETENTION now has equal priority to accuracy and visual quality.

This video should NEVER feel like:

- slow therapy content
- a calm educational explainer
- a long cinematic sequence with pretty shots
- a mental-health slideshow

It must feel like:

A FAST, IMMERSIVE, ESCALATING POV EXPERIENCE.

==================================================
RETENTION TARGET
==================================================

The title/premise must be fully understandable within the first 1–2 seconds.

The main title:

WHAT WEED-INDUCED
DEPERSONALIZATION
CAN FEEL LIKE

must remain visible long enough to read comfortably,
approximately 2.5–4 seconds,
while VISUAL ACTION is already happening underneath it.

Do NOT freeze the scene while the title is on screen.

The title and the story must begin at the same time.

==================================================
NO DEAD TIME
==================================================

There must not be any 3+ second section where the viewer receives no new information.

Every 2–3 seconds, at least ONE of these must happen:

- new visual clue
- new bodily sensation
- new audio change
- new internal thought
- new environment shift
- new question
- new escalation
- new social interaction
- new reality-checking behavior

The video should constantly move forward.

==================================================
FIRST 10 SECONDS — EXTREMELY IMPORTANT
==================================================

The first 10 seconds should contain:

0–2 sec:
TITLE + normal social scene + subtle sign that something feels off.

2–4 sec:
friend’s voice begins sounding distant / strange.

4–6 sec:
POV looks at hands.

6–8 sec:
first strong caption:
“You know those are your hands.”

8–10 sec:
second line:
“They just don’t feel like yours.”

By 10 seconds, the viewer must already understand:
- what the video is about
- what the first symptom feels like
- why it’s unsettling

Do NOT spend 10 seconds merely establishing the room.

==================================================
OPEN LOOPS
==================================================

Each section should create a reason to continue.

Examples:

At 8 sec:
“If your own hands feel unfamiliar… what happens when you look in a mirror?”

Then go to mirror.

At 25 sec:
“If your reflection looks correct… why does it still feel wrong?”

Then transition to derealization.

At 40 sec:
“If the world looks normal… why does it feel fake?”

Then move into time distortion / anxiety.

At 55 sec:
“The scariest part isn’t the feeling.”

Beat.

“It’s what happens when you keep checking it.”

Then start the loop.

Use this structure:
ANSWER → NEW QUESTION → ANSWER → NEW QUESTION.

==================================================
PATTERN INTERRUPTS
==================================================

Use visual or sound pattern interrupts approximately every 5–8 seconds.

Examples:

- sudden camera look-down
- mirror reveal
- friend enters frame
- environment audio drops out
- clock appears
- transition from room to hallway
- quick cut from hand to reflection
- muffled sound suddenly clears then fades again
- heartbeat enters
- time counter appears briefly

These must feel natural,
not random TikTok editing.

==================================================
PACE THE MENTAL EXPERIENCE VISUALLY
==================================================

Do NOT rely on captions alone.

SHOW:

- hand feels unfamiliar
- mirror feels emotionally disconnected
- friend’s voice feels far away
- room feels “too flat”
- depth feels strange
- time feels slow
- attention gets trapped
- repeated checking makes it worse

The viewer should be able to understand 70–80% of the experience even with sound off.

==================================================
CAPTION RULE
==================================================

Captions should be SHORT.

Maximum:
1 short sentence at a time.

Prefer:
4–9 words.

Avoid:
paragraphs.

Good:
“You know those are your hands.”

Bad:
“People experiencing depersonalization may report feeling disconnected from their body.”

Captions must hit emotionally, not academically.

==================================================
MIRROR SCENE RETENTION
==================================================

The mirror scene is a key retention moment.

Do NOT linger.

Structure:

approach mirror
→ reflection appears
→ hand raises
→ reflection matches
→ brief pause
→ caption:
“Everything looks right.”

Then:

“But it doesn’t feel like you.”

Then LEAVE the mirror scene quickly.

Total:
roughly 8–10 seconds max.

==================================================
DEREALIZATION SCENE RETENTION
==================================================

Do not just blur the world and hold.

Use layered changes:

first:
sound feels distant

then:
depth seems strange

then:
people feel disconnected

then:
time feels slow

then:
camera checks reality

This prevents one visual effect from becoming repetitive.

==================================================
ANXIETY ESCALATION
==================================================

Around the middle of the video, introduce a strong retention reset.

Preferred line:

“The feeling isn’t always the worst part.”

Pause.

“The panic can make it stronger.”

This should happen around:
45–55 seconds.

Then increase:

- heartbeat
- breathing
- checking behavior
- camera hesitation
- vignette
- sound isolation

This creates a SECOND ACT.

==================================================
THE LOOP — CLIMAX
==================================================

The reality-checking loop must be the fastest and most intense part.

Use rapid but readable 0.7–1.5 second cuts:

hands
mirror
clock
friend
phone
wall
hands again

Caption sequence:

“Do I feel normal?”

“Why don’t I feel normal?”

“Am I stuck like this?”

Then:

“The more you check…”

micro pause

“…the stranger it can feel.”

This is the main emotional payoff.

Do NOT drag this beyond approximately 10–12 seconds.

==================================================
ENDING RETENTION
==================================================

Do not let the ending collapse into a boring disclaimer.

The emotional resolution should come FIRST.

Preferred sequence:

POV sits down.
Friend quietly sits beside them.
Breathing slows.
The world looks more normal.

Caption:

“Nothing around you actually changed.”

Then:

“But it felt like everything did.”

THEN the small responsible note:

“If it continues after the high wears off, talk to a healthcare professional.”

Keep the note visually small and brief.

Do not end on a wall of text.

==================================================
TOTAL DURATION
==================================================

Target:
70–80 seconds if the story fits.

Do NOT force 90 seconds.

The oxygen video showed that long-form retention matters.

If a beat does not add something new:
cut it.

If the full story works in 72 seconds:
choose 72 seconds.

Do NOT keep 15 seconds of “cinematic breathing room” just because it looks nice.

==================================================
RETENTION REVIEW PROCESS
==================================================

After each preview, conduct a frame-by-frame retention audit.

Break into:

0–2
2–5
5–10
10–15
15–25
25–35
35–45
45–55
55–65
65–end

For EACH section answer:

1. What NEW thing does the viewer learn?
2. What visual event happens?
3. What question remains open?
4. Why should they keep watching another 3 seconds?
5. Is there any dead time?
6. Could this section be shortened?

If the answer to #4 is weak:
rewrite the section.

==================================================
SWIPE-AWAY TEST
==================================================

Use independent reviewers and ask:

“At which exact timestamp would you swipe?”

Do not accept:
“overall pacing is good.”

I want timestamped criticism.

Examples:

“6.2 sec — title is still on screen but nothing new happens”
“28 sec — mirror scene has overstayed”
“47 sec — derealization effect becomes repetitive”
“68 sec — ending feels complete before final disclaimer”

Then FIX the biggest dropout risks.

==================================================
RETENTION SCORE TARGETS
==================================================

Before final production, target:

HOOK >= 9/10
FIRST 5 SEC CLARITY >= 9/10
CURIOSITY >= 8.5/10
PACING >= 8.5/10
MID-VIDEO RESET >= 8/10
CLIMAX >= 8.5/10
ENDING PAYOFF >= 8/10
OVERALL RETENTION POTENTIAL >= 8.5/10

Do NOT manipulate scores.

If reviewers score lower, improve the actual video.

==================================================
CORE RULE
==================================================

Accuracy tells us WHAT to show.

Cinematography tells us HOW it looks.

RETENTION decides HOW LONG we stay on it.

Do not let any scene stay just because it is pretty.

This is a TikTok/Shorts production.

The viewer owes us nothing.

Every few seconds, earn the next few seconds.

Target 70–80 seconds, not 85–90.
Your oxygen video got 22.4s average watch time on 91s, so for this kind of subjective psychological video I’d rather make a tight 72-second banger than force it to 90 seconds.
And I’d structure the hook exactly like:
WHAT WEED-INDUCED DEPERSONALIZATION CAN FEEL LIKE
At first, everything feels normal.
friend says something → voice gets weird → camera looks at hands
Then your own body starts feeling unfamiliar.
````
